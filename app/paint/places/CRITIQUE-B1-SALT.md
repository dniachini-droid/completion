## Verdict

- **None of six reaches its bar.** The gallery itself now has the hall's hand (long wall, far glow, fog), and salt lit finally shows the salt's colour. What fails is again the look-at: each is a CG prop laid on the wall (a pencil line, a trough, a sausage of stones, a lift door, a wire ring), not something cut into or grown from the salt.
- **Neighbours that read alike:**
  - **cv-07, above the ring and the accepted b-2.A are one picture**: salt wall on the left, a ring, the tally rail running to a violet glow low right. cv-07 must be end-on and *very close* to the wall, as the brief says, so the wall fills half the frame as a surface running away; above the ring must look *up*.
  - **salt lit and cv-06 are the same split** from the same side, with the tally rail crossing it at the same height. cv-06 must be the salt face at arm's length, filling the frame, with the split as a dark chink and no rail.
  - Pick niche is the b-2.A wall with a trough added; acceptable as the same gallery, but the niche must read as cut, not attached.

## 1. pt-pl-w2-above-the-ring (5/10; unchanged) — out (round 3)

*A pencil line on a lit wall, with a wire ring under it.*
1. **The crack has lost its inside.** Round 2's stepped fissure is now a thin black zigzag slit drawn on flat salt (u .32–.45 v .38–.60): no depth, no lit lip, no narrowing into the dark. And the camera no longer looks up: this is the b-2.A view along the wall. Pitch up under the crack (VP inside it, as the brief says), crack two fingers wide with its walls stepping back into black.
2. **The pale thing is a ~6 px white fleck on the crack's edge** (u .38 v .47), and the brightest value is open salt beside it (137 at u .30 v .45), the same key pool round 2 asked to move. Set the sliver deep, with dark between it and the mouth, and let a `reach`-limited point make it the frame's peak; take the key's pool off the open salt.
3. The ring is a thin double-outlined wire circle floating on the wall (u .30–.38 v .52–.57); give it the rope-worn groove of b-2.A, or crop it to an arc at the bottom edge. The count beside the crack is five tiny ticks (u .43 v .52), barely there.

**What is still short:** looking up into a real crack with an inside, and one pale glint far back that is the brightest thing in the frame.

## 2. pt-pl-w1-pick-niche (6/10; unchanged) — out (round 8)

*A stone trough bolted to a salt wall.*
- Better: the frame is calm and the room is right: gallery wall, far glow, a quiet floor; beside the hall it is the same world.
1. **The niche reads as attached, not cut.** The sill is a separate rounded stone bar standing proud of the wall with a broken right end (u .55–.78 v .47–.52), and above it the recess is a black hollow whose top edge is torn like paper (u .55–.73 v .44–.47). A niche is a hole *in* the salt: sink the sill flush with the wall face, cut the mouth's edge from the salt itself (rough, soft), and light the inner floor so it has an inside.
2. **The strokes are on the sill's front face** as dark slots (u .58–.72 v .49–.50); the brief puts them on the lip. Put them on the top of the sill, as V-cuts lit on one flank.
3. **Brightest is far salt at the left edge** (155 at u .12 v .42); the niche peaks at ~110. Bring the rake light down so the sill's arris is the brightest line. The score marks above (u .50–.55 v .41–.44) are still five even parallel strokes, and the salt still shows no grey-and-white banding.

**What is still short:** a niche cut into the wall with a lit inside, strokes on its lip, the sill's edge the brightest line.

## 3. pt-pl-w3-salt-lit (5/10) — out

*At last the salt's colour; the split is a brown cutlet stuck on it.*
- Good: the warm light from behind turns the salt pink-banded and glowing (u .0–1 v .30–.55); p99 174 is the hall's. This is the gallery's colour, as the brief wants.
1. **The split reads as an object on the wall**: a pointed brown lump, flush with the salt, with a thin stalk at its top (u .40–.55 v .36–.65), like a pine cone or a slab of mince. No gap, no depth. Recess the stones into a dark split (a shadowed gap round them), rounder and fewer, each a water-worn pebble with its own soft highlight, and remove the stalk.
2. **The tally rail is a bright white cable tray** crossing the frame (u .0–1 v .47–.50), the second most eye-catching thing, and it cuts through the split. Tint it down to the salt's value, or pitch so it runs below the split.
3. Brightest is the salt beside the split (195 at u .35 v .52), and the stones peak far lower. Warm is 12.7 %, close to the limit; no glitter on the salt yet.

**What is still short:** round river stones packed in a dark split, the warm light catching their tops as the brightest thing; the rail quiet.

## 4. pt-pl-w3-far-end (4/10) — out

*A brushed-metal lift door with a nightlight beside it.*
1. **The door reads as metal or wood panelling**: fine vertical streaks over a flat slab (u .0–.75 v .20–.70), a seam up the middle. The brief says smoother and colder than the walls: make it plain dressed stone, huge, with only the faintest grain.
2. **No cup, no flame.** The "last cup" is a small brown egg on the side wall (u .90 v .30); the warm pool on the door is a soft smear with no source. Put the cup in frame, near and low to one side, with a flame as the warm peak, and let its light fall off fast up the face, so the edge where it gives out reads as a line.
3. **No cold haze** at the door's face or on the floor, and a stray blue spot on the door (u .25 v .60). The top is not lost; a dark lintel shows (v .15). Tilt up further so the face runs out of the frame.

**What is still short:** a warm flame close by, its light dying partway up a cold, immense stone face, with haze sliding down it.

## 5. pt-cv-06, the salt, close (4/10) — out

*The salt-lit split again, in cold light.*
1. **Not the brief's view**: the gallery wall at a few metres, a pointed brown lump with the tally rail crossing it (u .30–.60 v .05–.60). "The salt face at arm's length, filling the frame, VP into the split": come to arm's length so banded salt fills the frame and the split is a narrow dark chink going in, lit by thin cold shafts.
2. **The frost reads as white popcorn or foam** round the split's foot (u .40–.50 v .45–.55). Make it fine crystal growth in the lowest cuts: short needles, sparkling, not blobs.
3. The frame is dim (median 16, p99 89) and the lower half is black. Same picture as salt lit; see Sameness.

**What is still short:** salt as landscape: banded salt filling the frame, cold shafts through a chink, frost like needles as the bright note.

## 6. pt-cv-07, the tally, end on (6/10) — out

*A pretty wall, but b-2.A again.*
- Good: the value range is the hall's (p99 197), the far glow and fog are right, and the tally does run to a point.
1. **Not end-on, not close.** The camera stands a metre off the wall at an angle (ring at u .35 v .46, rail to u .80 v .50), which is b-2.A's view. Put the camera against the wall, looking straight along it, so the wall is a surface rushing away and the marks form one line of shadow converging on the VP.
2. **The tally is a rail with marks on its face** (u .0–.80 v .48–.62), a moulding, not marks cut into the wall. Cut the marks into the salt as a line of shadow.
3. **Brightest is a spotlight bloom on the wall** (237 at u .35 v .46), and the ring is a hard double-outlined loop floating on it. Make the ring's stroke the single clean line the brief asks for, with the light raking along the wall rather than pooled on it.

**What is still short:** end-on along the wall, one line of cut marks running to a point, the ring's stroke catching the rake.
