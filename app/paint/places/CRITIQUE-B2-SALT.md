## 1. pt-pl-w2-above-the-ring (5/10; unchanged) — out (round 4)

*A leaf-shaped cut-out in a moody wall.*
- Better: the camera now looks up; the lit diagonal band under a black upper wall is atmospheric, and the count is five clear lit ticks (u .64–.80 v .55–.60).
1. **The crack has no inside.** It is a flat black leaf shape with smooth outlines (u .44–.62 v .38–.82), like a hole punched in paper; no lit lip, no walls stepping back, no narrowing into dark. The VP is not inside it.
2. **The pale thing sits on the mouth, not far back**: a white oval on the crack's edge (u .49 v .55), and the frame's peak is open salt at the left edge (145 at u .03 v .51). Set it deep, with dark between it and the mouth, and make it the peak.
3. **The ring is a black rubber hose** at the bottom edge (u .10–.45 v .92–1), no groove, no worn stone. Give it b-2.A's worn ring or drop it.

**What is still short:** a crack with an inside, looking into it, and a pale glint far back as the frame's peak.

## 2. pt-pl-w1-pick-niche (7/10; up from 6) — in (round 9)

*A slot cut in the salt, lit from inside.*
- Better: the niche is now a recess in the wall (u .50–.74 v .44–.53): its left inner wall catches the violet, the sill sits in the mouth, and the strokes are on the sill's top. The room is b-2.A's, with the far glow deep down the gallery at the left; beside the hall it is the same world.
1. The niche is machined: straight top edge, a square inner corner and a flat plate jutting past the mouth on the lower right. Rough the mouth's edge and sink the plate.
2. The strokes are dark dashes, not V-cuts lit on one flank; the brightest thing is the far glow (153 at u .06 v .40) and the niche peaks lower. The score marks above (u .46–.49 v .43–.45) are still five even parallel strokes.
3. The upper wall's fog streaks read as smeared drapery (u .15–.60 v .05–.35). No grey-and-white banding yet.

**What is still short:** a rougher mouth, strokes lit as cuts on the lip, the sill's arris as the brightest line. Good enough to replace the stand-in.

## 3. pt-pl-w3-salt-lit (6/10; up from 5) — out

*Stones in a dark split, but boxed like a tray of eggs.*
- Better: the stones are now round brown pebbles packed in a dark gap, each with its own highlight (u .36–.55 v .43–.55), and the rail runs above the split, not through it.
1. **The split is a rounded rectangle** with a clean outline, filled edge to edge with same-size ovals: a tile or a sack, not a fissure. Make it tall and irregular, narrowing top and bottom, stones fewer and varied, some lost in the dark.
2. **The peak is a warm pool on bare salt above the split** (240 at u .39 v .44), a lamp hot-spot; the stones peak far lower. Let the warm light graze the stones' tops, not the wall.
3. The pink banding of round 1 is fainter, there is no glitter, and the rail (u .0–.95 v .37–.41) is still pale and the second thing you see.

**What is still short:** a natural split with fewer, varied stones, their tops the warm peak; the salt banded and glittering.

## 4. pt-pl-w3-far-end (4/10; unchanged) — out

*A hotel corridor corner with a wall sconce.*
- Better: there is now a cup with a real flame, and it is the warm peak (238 at u .74 v .38).
1. **No door.** The left two thirds is flat dark blue with nothing in it; the only surface is a panelled side wall lit gold (u .60–1 v .20–.75), which reads as wood trim. The brief's subject is the door's face rising out of frame.
2. **No edge where the light gives out**: the flame lights the side wall, not the door; nothing falls off along a face. Warm is 12 %, near the limit.
3. No cold haze. The camera looks into a corner, not up a face.

**What is still short:** the immense cold door face in front of the camera, the flame low beside it, its light dying up that face, haze sliding down.

## 5. pt-cv-06, the salt, close (5/10; up from 4) — out

*Banded salt with a torch shone on a crack.*
- Better: arm's length now; the salt banding fills the frame (bold, a little like fabric stripes), the rail is gone, and the frost at the split's foot is fine needles, not popcorn (u .45–.55 v .60–.72).
1. **The light is a round white spot** on the wall round the split (234 at u .57 v .59): a torch pool, not thin cold shafts through chinks. Keep the chink dark and let two or three thin shafts come out of it.
2. **The split is a thin black slit drawn on the surface** (u .48–.65 v .43–.68); the VP is not into it. Give it depth and a lit lip.
3. The lower quarter is floor and black with tally ticks (u .30–.40 v .78–.88); crop them, since they make this above the ring again (see Sameness).

**What is still short:** a chink going into the salt, thin cold shafts, frost needles as the bright note.

## 6. pt-cv-07, the tally, end on (7/10; up from 6) — out (round 2)

*Along the wall to the glow; almost there.*
- Good: the camera is now against the wall looking along it; the wall runs away to the far glow (the peak, 150 at u .50 v .51), with fog and the hall's value range. The tally is cut into the salt, no rail, and does run to the point.
1. **The near marks read as type**: a few fat, smudged glyphs ("F", "Γ") spaced wide (u .05–.40 v .57–.62), then a fizz of dots far off. Make them a regular run of narrow cut strokes, closer together, their shadows forming one line.
2. **The ring is still a double-outlined wire ellipse** (u .42–.46 v .40–.46). The brief's look-at is its single unbroken stroke: one cut line catching the rake.
3. The upper right has a swirl of smeared fog (u .75–1 v .30–.50) that pulls the eye off the vanishing line.

**What is still short:** one line of cut marks running to the glow, and the ring as a single clean cut catching the light.
