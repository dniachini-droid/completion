## Week 5

**pt-b-5.A, the tally from the head: PASS.** The bar-with-a-tick, ring, drop group reads clearly at the nearest record (u .25–.30, v .49) and repeats twice more down the wall, getting smaller. It reads as "the same three marks at the head of each record". The violet glow at the right edge is brighter than the marks and pulls the eye first, but it reads as the gallery's far end, not as a wrong object.

**pt-pl-w5-ledge-lip, the ledge's lip: PASS.** The small count (strokes over a base line) sits in the warm spill on the ledge's dark underside and reads as cut strokes. Nothing else in the frame competes.

**pt-pl-w5-worn-steps, the worn steps: FAIL.** The painter feared footprints; that is not the problem, since a stride-apart pair worn by feet is what the brief means. The problem is the opposite: the two dips are **perfect ellipses with hard, crisp rims** (u .15–.45 and .60–.85, v .60–.68), so they read as two round basins or sockets **cut on purpose**, not as stone worn down by use. That changes the clue from "something walked here, over and over" to "someone carved two dishes". Smallest fix: feather the rims (blend the hollow into the tread over about a third of its radius, no hard edge), make each dip slightly longer front-to-back than wide and deepest toward the step's front edge, and give the inside the same polish as the rail so it reads as worn smooth.

**pt-b-5.B, through a gap: FAIL.** The gap's edge is a **raised, ochre-brown rolled rim** proud of the wall, so the opening reads as a porthole or a pipe mouth fitted into the wall, not as a hole cut or broken through stone. Also, the square stone seen through it is drawn as a fine grid of small blocks with mortar lines, which reads as **brick or tile**; the only brick in the Site is the modern shaft, so this invites a false link. Smallest fix: remove the torus rim and its ochre tint; make the edge a plain cut through the wall's thickness, the reveal the same stone as the wall and lit on its lower inside by the lamps behind; and make the square stone beyond fewer, larger ashlar blocks (about a third as many courses), darker than the round stone as the brief says. The record on it ("the same three marks") is legible and should stay.

**pt-pl-w5-second-landing, the second landing: PASS.** A dark, crisply square opening at floor level in the lit end wall, clearly lower than the room: reads as a low square doorway. The lamp niche on the right reads as a lamp.

## Week 7

**pt-b-7.A, the lintel at the foot of the second flight: PASS.** The blank is a clean, pale, rod-shaped recess, the sharpest and lightest shape in the frame: it reads as a blank slot waiting for something. The painter's worry: the bar-with-a-drop reads as a stem with a teardrop hanging from its foot (like a plumb line), not as a pin; the two drops parted read as two drops leaning apart. Both marks are thin outline engravings, small at phone size, but they are not the look-at and they do not read as anything else.

**pt-b-7.B, the crew's wall again: PASS.** Twelve rings in a row and a thirteenth visibly smaller at the row's end, which also has the warm light on it (u .60–.65, v .50). Reads exactly as meant.

**pt-b-7.C, the great door open: FAIL.** The painter's worry is right. The lit steps run up the middle of the frame to a bright nosing (u .45–.65, v .48) and then stop at a **large black round opening** with a small violet glow in it (u .30–.80, v .20–.47). With the arched ceiling above, the picture reads as a **stair climbing up to a dark round portal**: the stair appears to end there and to go up, not down. "The light stops on the eleventh step and the stair goes on down into the dark" does not read. Smallest fix: continue the stair past the eleventh step as faint, cold, unlit step edges fading into the dark (four to six more visible nosings, dimming to nothing), with the walls and ceiling carried down at the stair's pitch instead of closing into a round black shape; remove the violet glow patch at the vanishing point. The last lit step should then read as a line where the light gives out on a stair that keeps going.

## Week 6

**pt-b-6.A, the side passage: FAIL.** The join does not read as stone meeting stone. The keyed square blocks at the sides are thin slabs proud of the wall with a streaky texture, lit warm, so they read as **wooden boards or plaques fixed to the walls** (u .05–.20 and .85–.95, v .35–.60); on the floor the teeth read as **mats or rugs** with a woven pattern (u .15–.60, v .60–.68). The round stone's curve is not visible, so there is no "two kinds of stone" in the frame, and the join is split to both edges with nothing at the centre. Smallest fix: yaw toward one side so one join fills the middle third of the frame; make the teeth flush interlocking stone (same cold stone colour as the square wall, the chisel texture, no streaks), with the round stone's curved face visibly meeting the flat square face at the joint; drop the patterned texture from the floor teeth.

**pt-pl-w6-square-gallery: PASS.** Small and dark as the painter feared, but the slope of broken stone is the only lit thing at the vanishing point (u .45–.55, v .50–.56) and reads unmistakably as a heap of broken blocks where the light ends. Flat ceiling and straight walls read.

**pt-b-6.B, the crew's wall: PASS.** Rows ruled across a tablet, a ring at the head of each row: the look-at reads as rows of rings, a ledger. Should fix (not a wrong reading of the look-at): the tablet is tan with printed-looking rulings in a neat dark frame, so its material leans toward a paper pad or a framed notice; shifting the surface to amber-grey with a low waxy sheen and making the rulings incised grooves would stop anyone taking it for her paper.

**pt-pl-w6-wall-shelf: FAIL (not found).** The level line with its two ticks is about 2 px wide at full size (u .31–.57, v .495), under 1 pt on a phone; the ticks are barely a pixel. The eye goes instead to the **violet spot of light just right of the shelf** (u .61, v .49), the brightest thing in the frame, which reads as a light or an opening with nothing in it. Smallest fix: widen the scratch to about 5–6 px and lift it to the palest value in the frame, make the end ticks at least 20 px tall, and move the violet catch-light onto the line (or cut it to half).

**pt-pl-w6-folder, her folder: PASS.** Small in a dark frame, but it is the only lit object, the slate sits on its cover, and HILL on the spine is legible. Reads as a folder (or thick book) under something low.

## Camp views

**pt-cv-01, the ladder's foot: FAIL.** The painter's worry is right. The lower walls are lit and the upper walls dark, so the shaft reads as a **level brick corridor**: the near wall reads as a floor, the far wall as a ceiling, the sky square as a lit doorway at the corridor's end, and the ladder as a **handrail** along the right wall. The chalk "↑ 40 FT" then reads as painted on a floor, pointing along the corridor. Smallest fix: light all four walls evenly from the sky square (daylight falls from above, so no wall is a "floor"), pitch to straight up so the square sits at frame centre with the four walls converging symmetrically, and make the ladder's rungs the clearest shapes in the frame (round bars standing off the wall, each lit on its upper edge, spacing shrinking toward the sky).

**pt-cv-02, the pipe: PASS.** The bootprints read at once as bootprints, a line of them worn down the floor of a round tube. Their rims are a little crisp (like stamped shapes) but nothing else is suggested.

**pt-cv-10, the landing: PASS.** One large ring cut deep and lit, set apart from smaller rings; reads as a carved ring, not a window (the wall shows through it).

**pt-cv-11, halfway down: PASS.** Four hollows, a space, four more, running up with the stair. With the lamp above-left in the frame, the lit lower inside and dark upper lip read as dishes pressed into the wall, not studs. Should fix: a touch of occlusion at the upper inner lip of each would make the concave reading certain at thumbnail size.

**pt-cv-12, the first turn: PASS.** "| 0.9 m" on the rail's worn top is legible when read along the rail (letters upright toward up-left); it does not read as 6.0 or anything else.

**pt-cv-13, the second flight: PASS.** "open. D9" in pencil on the jamb is legible; the full count of strokes above reads.

**pt-cv-14, the gap: PASS.** The look-at, the heap of square-edged chips, is lit and reads as a pile of small squared fragments below the opening. Should fix (not the look-at): the gap has the same ochre rounded-square frame as b-5.B and reads as a hatch or vent with nothing but dark blue in it; make it a plain cut through the wall with smoke-darkened square stone visible beyond, as in the b-5.B fix, so the two views agree.

**pt-cv-15, the join: FAIL.** The sliver in the joint (u .62–.66, v .43–.50) is **cream-coloured, soft and rounded**, so it reads as a bead of putty, caulk or wax, not metal; and at about 6 px wide it is easy to miss. Smallest fix: take the warm off it (cool dull grey, darker than the lit stone), give it a flat face flush with the joint and one sharp small specular pick of metal sheen, and make it about twice as long and fill the joint's full width so it is found.
