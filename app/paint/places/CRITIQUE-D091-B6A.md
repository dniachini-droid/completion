Earlier failures checked against: boards/plaques and mats (D085-1), outlined panels/window panes and a hanging stepped fixture (D085-2), teeth vanishing, tread plate, a fence of slats (POLISH-P3).

**Verdict: PASS**

- **Found.** The join is the warm-lit, brightest area of the frame, near the centre (u .38–.50, v .38–.65): the round vault's arch edge comes down onto a flat wall. At a 400 px-tall thumbnail it is still the first place the eye lands.
- **Reads as stone meeting stone.** The two stones are plainly different and plainly both stone: big smooth curved voussoirs on the round side, small coursed blocks with a pecked face on the square side, meeting along the arch's curve. The keying is modest (one pecked block set into the round wall at about u .33–.40, v .47–.53, one smooth voussoir lapping over the square face just above), but nothing in it reads as something else. None of the old wrong readings are back: no boards, mats, outlined panels, hanging fixture, tread plate or slats.
- **Painter's worry 1, pecked face as concrete block/stucco.** Close up the pecked texture leans slightly toward textured render; at phone size, with coursed joints, it reads as dressed stone blocks. Not a wrong reading. It is finer-coursed than the accepted `pt-pl-w6-square-gallery` walls (large plain faces, chisel-chevroned ceiling), a mild consistency difference only.
- **Painter's worry 2, far glow as a lit window.** The violet square inside the doorway (u .80–.90, v .43–.48) could be taken as a faintly lit window or opening at the back of a dark room. It is not the look-at and does not compete with the warm join; "light at the far end of a dark passage" is a fair reading. Not a failure.

**Should fix (optional, not blocking):** to make the clasp read as a clasp rather than a plain corner, add one or two more alternating teeth up the join (pecked block into the round side, smooth block into the square side), same values and textures as their own walls, no outlines; soften the far glow's hard square edge into a haze so it reads as depth, not a window.
