## A. Second attempts

**pt-pl-w5-worn-steps: PASS.** The hard-rimmed ellipses are gone. The nearest tread now carries two soft, polished hollows a stride apart (u .20–.45 and .60–.85, v .55–.65), and the joint line across the tread sags where it passes through each one, which says "the stone has sunk here". They read as worn by use, not carved. Should fix (not a wrong reading): the hollows are pale sheen with little shading, so at thumbnail size they could pass for two patches of light; a touch of shadow on the far inner slope of each would settle it.

**pt-b-5.B, through a gap: PASS.** The ochre torus is gone: the edge is now a dark, irregular cut through a thick wall (pointed break at the upper left, flat facets). The stone beyond is a few large ashlar blocks with the record legible on them. It no longer reads as brick, and it no longer reads as a fitted porthole. Should fix: a thin concentric groove still runs inside the reveal at the lower right (u .60–.72, v .52–.56), a faint echo of the old rim; delete it.

**pt-b-7.C, the great door open: PASS.** The wrong reading is gone. The lit steps run down to a bright last nosing (u .45–.60, v .45) and the stair visibly goes on past it as faint, cold nosings into a small dark tunnel, walls and ceiling carried along with it. The black band at the threshold (v .62) reads as the drop to the first step, a clear "going down" cue. The painter's worry: the darker oval in the vault (u .27–.87, v .08–.45) is a shadow on the ceiling above the stair, not an opening the stair runs into; the stair passes under it. Should fix: lighten that oval a step so it cannot be taken for a second opening.

**pt-b-6.A, the side passage: FAIL.** The warm streaky boards and floor rugs are gone, but the teeth still do not read as stone keyed into stone. On the left, the square blocks set into the round wall (u .10–.24, v .42–.62) are flat dark panels outlined by thin lines, so they read as **framed panels, plaques or dark window panes** fixed to the wall; at the top, the stepped block (u .40–.62, v .30–.37) reads as a **boxy fixture or an upside-down stair** hanging from the arch. The middle third of the frame is the dark square gallery, so the join is still at the edges, not the look-at. Smallest fix: remove the outline strokes round the teeth and give them exactly the surrounding stone's value and texture (square side chisel-marked, round side smooth), flush with both faces, so the joint shows only as a zigzag line where the two surfaces meet; flatten the top stepped block into the arch's face; yaw so one run of that zigzag joint (with the round wall's curve meeting the flat square wall) sits in the middle third, lit warm from behind.

**pt-pl-w6-wall-shelf: FAIL (wrong reading).** Now found (widened, pale, with ticks), but it reads as the wrong thing: the line has a rounded, shaded body and bulbous upturned ends that stand off the wall (u .37–.57, v .495), and it glows violet with a bloom at its right end, so it reads as a **towel rail or handle bar mounted under the shelf, or a strip light**, not a scratch in stone. Smallest fix: make it flush and incised: a flat pale line with no cylindrical shading and no cast shadow, a hairline of dark on its upper edge (the cut's wall), the end ticks as short cut strokes crossing the line; remove the emissive bloom at the right end (keep it the palest value by tint, not glow).

**pt-cv-01, the ladder's foot: FAIL.** The painter's worry is right. The walls are now evenly lit and symmetric, but a portrait one-point view of a square brick shaft still reads as a **level brick corridor with a lit doorway at its end**: the lower wall (with "↑ 40 FT" on it) reads as the floor, the sky square as a doorway, and the ladder, drawn as a run of rectangular frames standing off the right wall, reads as a **railing or fence** along the corridor. Smallest fix: give gravity a cue: pitch back from vertical (about 65–75°) so a strip of the shaft's floor with the ladder's feet resting on it shows at the bottom edge; put the ladder on the wall facing the camera as two straight rails with round rungs between them (each lit on its upper edge, spacing shrinking toward the sky); put the chalk on that wall beside the ladder's foot, reading upright.

**pt-cv-15, the join: PASS.** The cream bead is gone. The joint now carries a cool grey strip with one sharp small metal glint (u .62, v .48), the brightest small point in the frame, on the seam the eye already follows up. It reads as something metallic set in the joint. Should fix: lift the grey strip a touch lighter than the joint's shadow so its length, not only the glint, is seen.

## B. First attempts (redraws)

**pt-b-2.B, the rod: FAIL.** The light lies broadly across the blade's flat face (a warm glossy streak, u .20–.55, v .44–.50), not along a fine edge; the tip is **ragged and splintered** (u .17–.22, v .44), so the rod reads as a **polished stick or driftwood** (the B2 critique's wrong reading, still there); and the handle marks sit in evenly ruled cells along a line, which read as **ruler graduations**. Smallest fix: close the tip into a clean tapered point (no torn silhouette); keep the flat faces dark and matt and put the light only as a thin bright line along the one sharp edge; break the ruled line through the marks so they read as cut signs in cells, not graduations.

**pt-cv-03, the hall from the passage: PASS.** The lamp's small flame on the ledge is the brightest point and reads as a still flame on a clay lamp, with the hall running away into violet dark.

**pt-cv-04, the hall from the far end: PASS.** Carved rings on the upper walls where they curve into the vault are lit by the cups below them and read as rings; the lamp shows as a small warm point at the far end. Should fix (not a wrong reading): the brief puts the rings on the ceiling; tilt up a little so the lit rings sit higher in the vault.

**pt-cv-05, in the trough: PASS.** A small, dry brown leaf lying in a worn channel in the floor; reads at once as a leaf. The second trough is not clear, but it is not the look-at.

**pt-cv-06, the salt close: FAIL (not found, and a wrong reading).** There is no frost: the lowest cuts (the four strokes, u .47–.60, v .63–.68) are clean and empty. The eye goes to a **large round white glow behind the split** (u .40–.65, v .52–.62), which reads as a lamp or moon shining through a tear, not thin shafts through chinks. Smallest fix: remove the round pool; let the light come as two or three thin shafts from the split's chinks raking across the salt; fill the bottoms of the lowest cuts with fine white crystal needles (a sparkling fringe) and make that fringe the brightest value in the frame.

**pt-cv-07, the tally end on: PASS.** The lone ring (u .30–.35, v .41–.49) reads as one unbroken ring above the line of marks running to a point. Should fix: its lit rim has a glassy specular edge, so it can look like a hoop set on the wall; darken the inside of the stroke so it reads as cut.

**pt-cv-08, her cot: PASS.** "DAY 1" on pale tape on the cot's rail is lit and legible; boots below, the cot's sag visible.

**pt-cv-09, her shelf: PASS.** A clean, rod-shaped absence (pointed at the far end) in a grainy dust layer on the shelf: it reads as "something long lay here and is gone". Should fix: the near end shows a small vertical lip, so it edges toward a slot cut into the shelf; feather the dust into the stripe at its edges so there is no wall.

**pt-pl-w1-below-the-lamp: FAIL.** The niche is a neat round arch with a **bright, flat interior** that is the lightest thing in the frame, and the "dark ring" is a **raised ochre-brown collar** with a lip round the mouth (u .35–.67, v .46–.58): it reads as a **mousehole or a small oven with a clay rim** (the B2 wrong reading, still there), and the dark stain the brief names is not what the eye finds. Smallest fix: remove the collar's relief and its ochre tint, making the ring a flat soft-edged dark stain on the stone, darkest at the sill and fading outward, with a dull oily sheen on the sill; dim the niche's interior below the lit wall around the mouth, so the dark ring against the lamp-lit stone is the look-at.

**pt-pl-w2-above-the-ring: PASS.** A pale, rounded thing catching light inside a dark crack (u .48–.50, v .54–.57), the brightest small value in the frame, with the count beside the crack and the ring's arc at the bottom edge. Reads as "something pale inside the crack". Should fix: it sits close to the crack's mouth; push it a little further in so it reads as far back.

**pt-pl-w2-box-by-the-cot: PASS.** The mug shows its flat closed base on top, its rolled rim at the bottom against the slate, and the handle set toward the slate: a mug with no opening on top reads as upside down. Should fix (not the look-at): the box is dark, glossy and bellied, closer to leather or a cushion than soft card; matt pale grey card would settle it.

**pt-pl-w3-far-end: FAIL.** The cup's flame is on the right wall and its warm light spreads over the facing surface, but that surface does not read as the great door rising out of frame: the lit area's upper boundary is a **round arch of shadow** (u .20–.60, v .30–.36), so the lit face reads as an **arched alcove or doorway opening** at the end of a corridor, and the light fades softly sideways rather than giving out as an edge going up the face. Smallest fix: tilt up so the door's face fills the upper two thirds of the frame, its top out of frame; make the light's upper limit on the face a soft but clear, roughly level line well below the frame top, with the door's cold stone and haze visibly continuing above it; remove the arch-shaped shadow.
