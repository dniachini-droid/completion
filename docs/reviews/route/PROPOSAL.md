# The route: what went wrong, and the proper fix (D-153, D-154) — for Dan

> **Spoiler-free.** No story here: the detail is in the sealed record. The only areas named are the Mouth, the Lamp Hall, the Salt Gallery, the Survey Cut (and its new name) and the top of the Stair; everything else is "deeper areas".
> **Stage 1 of 2.** This is the audit and the design. Nothing in the app or the story has changed yet. If you approve this, Stage 2 builds it.

_2026-10-05. Checked by an independent reviewer who had no part in the design; their findings are fixed below._

---

## 1. The honest verdict: it was not intentional

You were right. **Movement through the game was never designed.** It is the by-product of how the story was planned:

- **The story was planned one week at a time.** Each week was a list of what it had to show: which symbols you learn, and which piece of each old record you read.
- **Each week's five places went wherever that week's things happened to be.** The order inside a week was simply the order of the list.
- **The extra places were chosen to put something locked in view at the end of each day.** The locked things are spread over every area, so these extra places hop around too.
- **Every place costs the same walking effort.** So each week's places had to be "somewhere", and that often meant the top long after the way down had opened.
- **Two records you read a little of each week are at the top of the map.** So every week pulled you back up, however deep you'd got.
- **In the story you sleep by the lamp in the Lamp Hall every night, but the app never shows it.** That's the natural reason to see the top again. Without it, a trip back up looks like a jump, and the next day's place below looks like another.
- **The game has no idea of travel.** "Where you are" is just the last place reached. When a place wasn't ready, the game skipped to the next one on the list, whatever area that was in.
- **Nobody ever checked movement.** Every review looked at pacing, facts, fairness and the writing, never at how you get from one place to the next.

The places themselves are sound, and the map behind them is coherent: every area joins the others in a way that makes sense. **What was never designed is the path between them.**

### The numbers (weeks 1–14, 68 places, as you'd actually play them)

I walked a simulated player through all 14 weeks: ordinary weeks, slow weeks and big weeks. All three meet the places in the same order. You change area **48 times**:

| How the move reads on screen | Count |
|---|---|
| There's a reason, and the screen tells you | 14 |
| There's a reason, but the screen never says (no "you go back up…") | 16 |
| No reason at all: the place was there to fill the week | 18 |

- **15 area changes in the first 20 places.** At worst, 16 in 20.
- **10 times** you go out to an area and straight back.

## 2. Why it's hard to tell where you are: how the app shows location

I went through every screen that names or pictures where you are. The game has **areas** (like the Salt Gallery) and **places inside them** (a niche, a far end, a doorway). The screens mix the two:

1. **The same big title means an area on one screen and a place on another.** The Map's titles are areas. Today's title, the arrival screen and the delve screen show a place. The screen after your first word shows an area, but styled like a place.
2. **Eight places have the same name as their area.** So "The Lamp Hall" is both the area and a place inside it.
3. **Nothing tells you which area a place is in, or that you've just entered a new area.**
4. **On the Map, the area you're standing in hides its own places.** Every other area lists them, and they sit out of sight at the bottom of a small box.
5. **Links that do the same thing look different.** This is your "different looking links to get into each location":
   - Today's title re-reads a place, but looks like plain text.
   - The Map uses small inline names with camp views mixed in.
   - Locked things are separate rows with their own wording.
   - In the Daybook, locked things can be tapped but places can't.
6. **Locked things look like places,** side by side in the same list.
7. **Area names drift.** One area appears under three different names on different screens.
8. **Camp is invisible.** You sleep by the lamp every night, yet a short day says "You make your camp at…" somewhere else.

## 3. The fix: a journey you can follow

### The shape of the whole journey (not week by week)

The place is **a descent with a home at the top**:

- **Home** is the Lamp Hall, where you sleep by the lamp every night. It has two side rooms: the Survey Cut (renamed, below) through the doorway in its side wall, and the Salt Gallery round its far corner.
- **One way down** runs from home through the top of the Stair, a stage at a time, into the deeper areas and on.
- **Side branches** open off the way down. Each is entered when you reach it, walked, and left.
- **Later, two ways down meet,** and the descent goes on as one.

How it should feel:
1. The first weeks explore home, until your first word opens the way down.
2. Then you go down a stage at a time.
3. You come back up each night.
4. Once in a while, on the way home, you turn off because something you've just learned asks you to.

### The movement principle: every place is one of four kinds, and you always know which

1. **On:** the next place along the way. Most places are this.
2. **A new area:** the first place in an area. It gets its own arrival: the area's name, one line on how you got there, then the place.
3. **An evening at home:** something at the top worth looking at tonight, because of what you learned today, or something small found by lamplight round camp (at most one a week).
   - It shows as that night at camp.
   - It **doesn't move where you are**, and **costs no walking.**
   - It comes once a night, when you say goodnight. If you skip goodnight, it comes the next time you open the app ("Last night, at camp"). It only comes after a day's work is done; just opening the app earns nothing.
4. **A turn-off on the way home:** an area you've walked before, revisited on the way back up only because something you learned below asks for it. Its first sentence says what.

**Rules that hold it together:**
- **No more skipping into another area.** If a place isn't ready, the little story moments it waits for play on the way, as long days already do.
- **The notebook and record moments at the top become evening scenes,** instead of popping up between two jobs while you're deep down.
- **"Where you are" is the last place you walked to.** Evenings never change it.
- **A day that ends short of a place says where you turned back,** not where you "camped". You always sleep by the lamp.

### What changes in the story

- **The truth, the ending and the order you learn the symbols are all untouched.**
- **Most changes re-order places inside a week.** A few places move to a nearby week, always earlier, never later.
- **One real change.** In the later weeks, one approach is reversed: you walk on instead of doubling back. That changes one small physical detail of where something is cut. It's recorded under the project's retcon rule, with everything else about that place kept.
- **About 80 lines are touched:**
  - a reason sentence for each evening and turn-off;
  - lines that assumed the old order;
  - one line for each area saying how you get there;
  - about ten place names;
  - every "Survey Cut".

  They go through the usual writing process with cold readers.
- **No new paintings.** Every place keeps its own.

## 4. How location will look in the app

- **One naming rule everywhere: "Area · place"**, for example "The Salt Gallery · the pick niche". No place shares its area's name, and each area has one name only. "Her camp" becomes "her room", so "camp" only ever means yours, by the lamp.
- **Today's title is the area,** and it changes only when you move area. The place is the small line under it. Tapping it re-reads where you are, and it looks tappable.
- **Arrivals have four looks, one per kind:**
  - **A new area:** "A new area", the area's name large, how you got there, its picture, then the place.
  - **The next place in the same area:** "Arrived", the area small above, the place as the title.
  - **A turn-off:** "On the way back", the area small above, the place as the title.
  - **An evening:** "Tonight, at camp", lamp-lit, followed by the bedtime line. If something locked is there, you can use a Key on it right there.
- **The Map stays one map** (your call, D-092):
  - The areas are drawn as the descent: home at the top with its two side rooms attached to the Lamp Hall, the Stair as one line down, the branches off it.
  - **Camp** is marked by the lamp. **Where you are** is marked separately.
  - Tap an area to see its name, **every place in walking order** (including the one you're in, marked), then its locked things in their own list.
  - **Every row looks the same:** a name and its state ("here", "read again", "needs a Key", "use a Key"). Places come first, so you always see them. The next place is marked on its area.
- **The delve and the road line** say "Further into the Salt Gallery" or "On down". They never name a place before you reach it, and they always point at the next place you'll walk to.
- **Everywhere else uses "Area · place" too:** Welcome back, the lock screen, the Daybook (where places become tappable, and each day shows that night's evening) and records.
- **The screen after your first word** becomes a proper "new area" arrival for the Stair.

## 5. The new name for the Survey Cut

**The Box Room.** It says plainly what it is: a small spare room off the hall, full of someone's things. Its own first line already describes it that way. It never gets mixed up with your camp by the lamp, and it gives nothing away. The one thing in it that would clash is "the box by the cot", which becomes "the shoebox by the bed".

**Runner-ups:**
- **Her Room:** warm, but it leans on a person before the first week has shown one.
- **The Den:** plain and homely, but lighter than the world's voice.

**Dropped:**
- **The Cot Room:** in British English a cot is a baby's bed.
- **The Side Room:** too close to the name of an area you'll meet later.

## 6. Your save

- **Everything you've reached stays reached, and nothing you've seen plays again.**
- "Where you are" is worked out from the places you actually walked to. So if the last thing you saw is now an evening at home, the app still puts you where you really were. No teleporting.
- **Each place's kind is worked out when it plays,** from where you've been, so your save never gets the wrong kind of arrival.
- **Anything that moved earlier and that you haven't reached simply comes next,** with its reason. No line assumes which place you saw before it.
- **This is proven:**
  - on every sample save;
  - on saves made under the old order, stopped at every place in weeks 1–6 (so wherever you are now is covered), plus mid-week in weeks 7, 13 and 14;
  - each one walked on to week 14, with and without saying goodnight, and on big days that cross an evening.

## 7. One choice for you: the pace

Evenings no longer cost walking, so 53 of the 68 places are on foot instead of all 68. With the same effort, you'd reach the story's places about a fifth sooner. Two options:

- **(a) Keep the walk between places as it is** (my recommendation). A normal day still reaches a place, and the story just runs a little faster. It never waits.
- **(b) Lengthen the walk** by about a quarter, so the old balance of effort to story holds.

## 8. How we'll know it's right (the acceptance test)

1. **Rule tests:**
   - every place can be reached, with no stalls;
   - every move is one of the four kinds, and every evening and turn-off states its reason;
   - no out-and-straight-back without a stated turn-off;
   - evenings cost no walking and come once a night;
   - old saves carry on cleanly;
   - all the existing tests still pass, with any changed number named.
2. **A real playthrough of the built app** on a phone-sized screen (390 × 844):
   - A fresh save is walked through all 14 story weeks, capturing every arrival, every evening, Today's title and the Map at each move.
   - Then the same from saves at your possible points under the old order.
3. **A fresh reviewer** who had no part in the design reads that whole journey as a player and judges every move:
   - Do I know where I am?
   - Do I know why I moved?
   - Does each area read as a place with places inside it?

   Any move that fails is fixed and the walk runs again, until every move passes.

## 9. The work (Stage 2), and its size

1. The sealed story documents first (they are the source of truth), then the lines, through the writing process.
2. The story data: the order, the weeks, and each area's name, picture and "how you get there" line.
3. The engine:
   - kinds worked out as places play;
   - "where you are";
   - evenings off the walking meter, once a night;
   - no skipping into other areas.
4. The screens: Today's title, the four arrival looks, the Map's drawing and its area box, one link style, the road and delve labels, and the naming on every other screen.
5. Tests, the full playthrough and the reviewer's loop, then an independent review, then you.

**Size:** the biggest change since weeks 8–14 went in. A few days of focused build and test work, plus the writing rounds and the reviewer loop. One branch, one pull request, and nothing merged without your OK.

**One thing to watch:**
- In weeks 3 and 4, a lot happens at home in the evenings: the first nights with the hall lit, and some reading in the Box Room and the Salt Gallery.
- Meanwhile the way down moves one stage a week.
- The walking never waits on those evenings. The reviewer will say if it reads as being made to stay at the top, and if it does, it changes.
