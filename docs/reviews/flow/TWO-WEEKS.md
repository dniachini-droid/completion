# Flow review: two weeks lived in the app, played like Dan

**How this was done.** A fresh save was played from Thursday 1 October to Thursday 15 October on a phone-sized screen (390 × 844, touch), with the phone's clock moved forward through each day the way a real fortnight would pass: mornings, work, evenings, bedtimes, one weekend with nothing done, a late night, a very long work day, three days away, a day of ticking things off afterwards, an errand run, an avoided job, jobs added as they came. Every screen seen was recorded (text structure and a picture). Scripts and about 200 screenshots are in `scratchpad/review/life/` (`d01.mjs` … `d15.mjs`, `shots/`). Nothing in the repo was changed. No story text is quoted here and no story names are used: "the first place", "a locked niche" and so on.

The spoiler rule means some findings are described a little abstractly; the day and screenshot names let a developer find the exact screen.

## Findings, ranked by how much they would hurt Dan

### A. Confirmed bugs

**A1. The day's finish line moves after he does the jobs it showed him.** (Day 4; `shots/d04-02-today.png` vs `d04-14-today-evening.png`.) Sunday morning Today listed three jobs under "Today" and two under "If there's time". Dan did the three. The day did not finish: the done tank clean dropped to "If there's time" and the two others rose into "Today", so the gold "done, and it was enough" never came although he had done exactly what he was shown, three hours' worth. Why: tank clean was on the list because it was due (fortnightly), not because the week's plan put it there; the finish-line rule keeps a done job on the line only if it came from the plan, so the moment it was done it fell out and the line refilled with the next undone jobs. This directly undercuts the one promise of the Today screen ("do these and the day is done") and reads like moving goalposts. Fix: any job that was on the day's list when it was done stays on the finish line (not just jobs the plan placed); the line should only ever shrink or stay, never refill because of a completion.

**A2. The word's screen cannot be left.** (Day 14; `d14c-02-cut.png`, `d14c-03-after-later.png`, `d14c-04-after-today-arrow.png`.) When the first word is reached, the cutting screen opens. Its "Later" link and the "Today" arrow at the top both bring the same screen straight back, because "later" goes to Today and Today redirects to the unseen arrival. Dan is trapped until he guesses a symbol and performs the cut. In my run he reached it at 15:00 after ticking off a job; if he had reached it at 23:50 or on the bus he would have had to do a four-step ritual to see his list. Fix: "Later" (and the arrow while uncut) should take the "stay" route the code already has for Today, and the cut should wait on Today as a quiet line ("A word waits to be cut") until he chooses.

**A3. "Use it on the Map" points at nothing.** (Days 1–6; `d02-04-map-key.png`, `d06-04-map.png` … `d06-08-map-node-3.png`.) From the moment the first Key was kept, Today said "1 Key · Use it on the Map". The Map, visited on Friday and again on Tuesday, tapping every light, offered no "Use a Key" and showed no "needs a Key" anywhere. Five days later the Key was spent by itself on arrival. Why: the Today link appears when the rules say some locked thing could be opened, but the Map lists only locked things the story has already pointed out; the one in question had not been pointed out yet. The two screens disagree, and the step screen seconds earlier had said "nothing locked is within reach yet", so Dan is told three different things in a minute. Fix: show "Use it on the Map" only when the Map will actually list something openable (same filter as the Map's "needs a Key"); otherwise say "You keep it for the next locked thing you come to" and nothing more.

### B. Clumsy flow (not bugs, but it does not flow)

**B1. Keys: earned, announced, and then always spent for him.** Over two weeks Dan earned six Keys (lesson twice, meal prep, tank clean, Spanish study, and one more on the probe day). Every one was either used instantly where he stood or kept and then spent automatically at the next arrival ("You used a Key you were keeping", once twice on one screen). He never once chose. Meanwhile the Map has shown one locked thing "needs a Key" since day 7 that none of those Keys went to. The telling is now honest (finding of D-141 holds), but as a game mechanic Keys have no decision in them: they are a reward message, not an item. Suggestion: when a Key is earned and something is openable both here and on the Map, ask once ("Use it here, or keep it?"); or simply let a kept Key never be auto-spent, so the Map's "Use a Key" is the one place Keys are used.

**B2. The "Ahead" paragraph on Today describes a thing behind him.** (Days 2–4; `d04-02-today.png`.) After moving on from the second place, Today's heading named the new place while "Ahead" still described a locked thing at the previous one, for three days. The label says Ahead; the content is behind. Suggestion: label it by where it is ("Behind you, still locked" / "At <place>") or show only things at or beyond the current place.

**B3. Two full screens before Today after an absence.** (Day 12.) Coming back after three days, Dan gets the welcome-back screen, then the week's Daybook page with its offer, then Today. Both are good screens; together they are a lobby he must walk through when the stated aim is "one small task". Suggestion: after an absence show the welcome back only, and put a quiet "A page was written for you · Daybook" line on Today for the week close.

**B4. Plan it for me / Look ahead leaves him three taps from Today.** (Days 5 and 12.) Finishing the look-ahead or "Plan it for me" lands on the Week; the arrow says "Daybook" and goes back to the Daybook page (offer gone); then Today. Suggestion: after planning, the arrow should go to Today, or the Week should offer "Today" directly.

**B5. The errand run's end is a wall.** (Day 6; `d06-18-errand-end.png`.) After a 45-minute errand run the end screen stacked: the run's summary list, a done line, a long story paragraph, four symbol guesses (each with four word buttons) and two finds, then "See where you are" to a new place with more words. That is a lot to read and answer in one go after posting letters. Suggestion: at most one guess per end screen; defer the rest to the Symbols page (which already says "Seen, but not known yet").

**B6. Today never finished on day 1 because of a 25-minute avoided job.** Three hours of real work (course, gym, lesson) and the day stayed violet because the avoided one-off, first on the list, was untouched. The rule ("every job on the line") is deliberate and not punishing, but the avoided job is exactly the one most likely to remain, and it was placed first. Suggestion: avoided one-offs should not block the finish line on their first day (treat them as "If there's time" when the line already holds about three hours), or place them last.

**B7. The Map's "you are here" and the box disagree in name.** (`d14c-16-map.png`.) The marker sits on a light named for a stretch while the box underneath names the current place; both are correct, but a glance reads as two different places. Minor labelling: "you are here · <place>" under the light.

### C. Minor polish

**C1. Step screen then Today contradict within seconds on Keys** (covered by A3): "nothing locked is within reach" → "Use it on the Map".

**C2. The errand pick list offers non-errands** (Spanish study, a 1-hour study session, under "On today"). Harmless but odd; perhaps exclude recurring jobs.

**C3. Two records share one title in Records** (`d07-23-records.png`). They may be different sheets, but to the reader it looks like a duplicate; add a distinguishing word.

**C4. A fortnightly job kept up gives no Key the second time and says nothing** (day 14). Rule-correct (one Key per period), but the earlier Key telling has taught Dan "kept up → Key"; a quiet "already earned this fortnight" would stop him wondering.

**C5. A passed appointment sits on Today unremarked** (day 8, lesson "18:00" at 22:00). Fine by rule 9, but a small "went by" note would read as noticed rather than ignored.

**C6. The ring on end screens is caught mid-count in the first second** ("1 minutes" in a screenshot) — the count-up is lovely; only the grammar at 1 is off.

**C7. "Go down" from the descent screen goes to the Satchel** to choose a job, even when the day is already gold. Reasonable, but the arrow from the Satchel then says "Back" and returns to the descent screen; a clearer destination name would help.

## What works well

- **The next action is obvious on opening, every day.** Today always shows the list, one big button, and in the evening the gold Go to sleep rises above the list. The core rule holds across all 15 days, with the single exception of A2.
- **Nothing accumulates.** Old jobs do not linger on Today; avoided one-offs slide to the Satchel and come back only when placed or done; the Satchel held two lines after two weeks; the Week folds past days into "3 done". No pile-up, no red, no counts of missed things.
- **Failure is never punished or shamed.** Late nights ("Nothing is lost"), a day with nothing done (no comment), a forgotten lesson (no comment), three days away ("the road has waited for you"), Not today ("Taken off today. Put it back"): every one of these is handled with exactly the right words.
- **Minutes are honest and consistent.** The road line on Today, the set-up's route, the delve's ring, the Done screen's count, the Daybook's tallies and the Week's "about 2 h" all agreed across the fortnight. 30 minutes' work took 30 off the next place every time; places came every 150 minutes with no stall on the 8-hour day; a late delve counted for the right day.
- **Ticking off afterwards is as good as a delve**, with the same story moment, and "Not done after all" → "No more" pays nothing twice.
- **The errand run itself** (pick, strike off, "What got done?", per-errand share) is a genuinely good piece of design for a real trip out.
- **Tonight** (bedtime, tomorrow's first job, one line for the mind) is light and optional; the morning find after a kept bedtime is a small, real pleasure.
- **The welcome back** is one quiet screen that says the right thing.
- **No task farming found.** A 10-minute "I can't start" delve earned 10 minutes and a question, not a place. Trivial inputs never out-earned real work. No productivity theatre either: in two weeks I never had to administer anything; the Week laid itself out and was right enough to ignore.

## Verdict: is it a game?

Yes, mostly, and more than it was a month ago by the record. The loop — do a real thing, watch the flame move, arrive somewhere, read, guess, keep a Key — holds across a fortnight, and the places kept coming at a pace that rewarded long days without punishing short ones. The three places it stops being a game and becomes a system Dan has to argue with are A1 (the finish line moving after he did what it asked), A2 (a screen he cannot leave), and the Keys (A3/B1: told to use something he cannot, then having it used for him). Fix those three and the fortnight is seamless; the rest is polish.
