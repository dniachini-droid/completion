## 1. Thematic core

**The dead are not waiting for an answer. They are waiting to be read.**

Everything in the story is a version of that sentence. A vanished people sent their language out as a gift and were misread, across ages, as a question. The antagonist has spent longer than human history trying to *answer* his dead, when what they left him was something to *read*. The lives in the records each did one unfinished thing and never saw what it became; the player is the one who reads all of them, and so keeps them. The ending is the difference between keeping and answering.

A second theme runs underneath, never stated in the fiction and never addressed to Dan: **the way down is by going out.** The deep doors of the place open only to someone who keeps returning from the world. The one who never leaves cannot go deeper. That is the fictional meaning of a Key, and it is the reason the antagonist needs a reader at all.

Tonal rule: the world is dark (a civilisation ended; something listens; people gave their lives to a wall), the voice to Dan is kind (P14), and every dark room is paired with something loved: a lamp left lit, a joke in a tally, a woman who wrote a lesson for a stranger.

## 2. World rules

1. **The Cut is real technology.** The script is not a natural language. Its signs, cut into the right stone and sounded, *act*: they make light, open doors, move stone, wake devices, hold things. "Ancient technology that looks like magic": explain meaning, never mechanism.
2. **Words work only where the stone is laced.** The Site's rock is threaded with the material of the Seed (§4). Cut a word into unlaced stone and nothing happens. This bounds powers to the Site and to things made from its stone (the cutting rod, the clay lamp glazed with its dust). It also explains why every reader who tried to take a word *out* found it weak or dead.
3. **To use a word is to sound it.** A word cut and sounded at ordinary strength acts locally. A word cut and sounded *loudly* (at scale, or at the Seed, or with the intensifier sign) carries. Carrying is what draws the Listeners (§4). The Builders' whole architecture exists to let the language be used without carrying: the Quiet is a place built to be *quiet*.
4. **The Site never lies.** Physical evidence, the mechanics of the script, and anything the Site does are always true. Only writers can be wrong, biased, selective or lying. The Custodian omits; he never lies (this is a character rule and a fairness rule).
5. **Nothing waits.** Every writer in the records is finished before Dan arrives. Nothing in the Site decays, grieves or worsens in his absence. Dust, not decay. No text ever refers to the reader's lateness.
6. **The deep locks count returns.** The Builders' safeguard: the deep doors ("counting doors") open only to one who keeps leaving and coming back with the world on them. This is the fiction for Keys (earned by living the week). A door that lacks its count simply waits.
7. **Reading changes nothing in the world; it changes the reader.** Progress is excavation, never intervention. Verbs for reward: found, read, learned, understood. Never saved.
8. **Ages are unnamed.** The records are set in our own world's past, but no age, nation, city or date is ever named. Objects, trades and units carry the era: bronze sickle, measuring cord, blasting powder, field notebook. Dan works out the age.

## 3. The central player fantasy

*"Every real hour takes me further down, and what I find was left for whoever came next."*

Dan is the latest reader of a place that has been read before. He inherits the lamp, the rod, the lessons and the unfinished work of everyone who came before him, and he is the first who can read all of it, because he is the first who keeps going back up.

## 4. Cosmology

There are four kinds of thing in the story's universe. Only two of them are ever seen.

**The Speakers.** A people, very far away, long dead, of unknown form. Their civilisation learned late that anything that speaks loudly enough is found and taken. At their end, they did the one thing left to them: they made their language into a message that carried *them* (their concepts, their grammar, their way of dividing the world, a compressed self-portrait) and sent it in every direction as an inheritance, with a warning folded inside it. The message travelled as matter, not light: many small dense **seeds**, one of which fell into this world's crust in deep time and *cut itself* into the rock around it. The Speakers are never seen. They are known only through their language, which is the game's script, and through what the Builders inferred about them. (Authorial truth: the message was sincere and sent before they were reached. It is a gift, not bait.)

**The Listeners.** Whatever takes. Never seen, never described. Known only by three effects: the Speakers ended; the Builders ended after they used the language at scale; and small physical anomalies followed every loud use of a word in human ages (a well going still, compass needles turning to the hill, a sound in the sky "like a door in a very large house"). Their nature is a mystery the story deliberately never settles. Three theories exist in the records: the Custodian's (a gathering mind: to be taken is to be *kept*, to join), the Builders' majority view (a hunger: to be taken is to end), the Linguist's (a process, not a mind at all; nothing is "in" it). **Authorial truth, never confirmed on the page:** the Linguist is right. The Listeners are a process, like fire. Nothing taken persists. The Custodian's hope is false.

**The Builders.** A deep-time civilisation of this world, before humans. Not human. Few, long-lived, dwellers of the deep rock: near-blind, large-eyed, long-fingered, reading by touch and sound, patient over centuries. They found the seed's cut rock and learned to read it, then to cut, and the language gave them power (light without fire, stone that moved, doors that answered, wounds that closed). They grew great by it. They used it loudly. They were found. Their end came over roughly a generation as a long quietening, and their records stop. One of them survived, because he was in the deepest, most bound part of the great work they had begun: the place the game calls the Site.

**The readers.** Humans, across the ages, who found the Site and learned some of the Cut. They are the linked lives of the records. All are finished. Dan is the last.

## 5. History (deep to recent; full chronology in `TIMELINE.md`)

1. **The fall of the seed.** A seed struck this world's crust in deep time and wrote itself into the rock around it: a mass of glassy, fused stone, densely cut, with the language spreading outward through the rock as a thin lacing. This is the **Seed-place**, at the bottom of the Site.
2. **The Builders find the cut stone.** Generations pass in learning to read it. A reading is confirmed when the rock *answers*: the first word to work was FIRE-GIVE (light). The Builders become a people of the word.
3. **The great age of the Builders.** They cut the language into their halls and their tools; they move stone, open the deep, heal, build. They use it loudly and at scale.
4. **The warning is read.** Deep in the seed's text, late, the Builders read the folded warning: *what speaks loudly is found.* Some of the anomalies they had noticed (the rock going still, sounds in the deep) are understood.
5. **The split.** Two minds among the Builders. The **binders** hold that the language must be kept and used only quietly, in one bound place, as a school and a warning. The **answerers** hold that the Speakers must be answered: that being found is being joined, that the Speakers *are* the Listeners now, that to answer is to go home. The binders prevail. The great work begins: the Quiet, a place cut so that words act inside it without carrying, with counting doors that open only to one who returns from the surface (so that nothing that lived only in the dark, whatever they might become, could open it), and a last binding word prepared for the Seed itself, to be cut when the work was finished.
6. **The end of the Builders.** Before the binding is cut, the long quietening comes. The Builders end. The chief binder, the architect of the Quiet (the Custodian, §7), is in the deep, and survives.
7. **The long solitude.** Alone for an age, he uses the word REMAIN on himself: KEEP-ONE-HERE. It keeps him alive and it keeps him *in* the Site; over ages it makes him more stone than flesh. He cuts his people's names into the walls. He comes, slowly, to believe that the answerers were right.
8. **The readers.** Humans reach the Site by accident in age after age: a salt-cutter after an earthquake; a surveyor running a tunnel; an engineer blasting for a railway; a woman looking for the salt-cutter's lamp. Each meets him. Each is taught a little. He never stops any of them. He records each life in the Cut, in the reader's own words, as a lesson for the next. Some of them use words loudly; each loud use is followed by an anomaly; each reader, sooner or later, stops returning to the surface, and the deep doors close to them.
9. **The Linguist.** The most recent reader. She got furthest, understood the most, wrote her own lessons in the Cut for whoever came next, and chose to remain. Her last notes hold both words: the binding and the answer, and the sentence "I do not know if he is right."
10. **Dan.** The next reader. The lamp is already lit.

## 6. The hidden truth (the answers)

**What is the Site?** Not a tomb, not a temple, not a machine that wants something. It is a *hold*: a place built by the Builders to keep a dangerous inheritance quiet and readable, a school with a warning at the bottom. Its working name in the open docs, "the Quiet," is literally what it is.

**Who is the tall figure in every age?** The Custodian: the last Builder, architect of the Quiet, kept alive and in place by the word REMAIN, which over ages has made him part of the walls. Each age names him differently. His ringed name in the Cut is KEEP-ONE: *the one who keeps*. The Linguist translated it as *Custodian*.

**Who wrote the records?** He did. The lives are his transcriptions: he asked each reader to tell their life, and he cut it, faithfully and selectively, as a lesson for the next reader. That is why every record has his opening formula, why he appears in all of them, and why the archive has a curator. The exceptions are in the readers' own hands (the Surveyor cut some marks himself; the Linguist wrote everything herself), and the difference in hand is a clue.

**Why has he never stopped anyone?** Because he wants a reader to reach the Seed. He cannot do it himself: his own counting doors exclude anyone who does not return from the world, and he can never leave. Every gate Dan opens is a gate the Custodian wanted open. He is not the obstacle. He is the help. That is the dread.

**What does he want?** To answer. He believes his people were not ended but *taken up*, that the Listeners are a gathering mind, that to be found is to be joined, and that this world will be found anyway as its surface grows louder every age. His cure: be found on purpose, whole, together, by cutting the answering word (VOICE-GIVE-FAR) at the Seed. He is right about the problem (there is something that takes; the language does carry; readers have drawn it before) and wrong about the cure (nothing persists in it; the surface's noise is not the language and has never drawn anything; only the Cut carries).

**What are the name-rings on every wall?** Dan sees ringed names on nearly every surface from week one and, for most of a year, reads them as decoration or as the "signatures" of the place. They are the names of the Builders. He cut them, over ages, one by one. The walls of the Site are a memorial written by the antagonist. **This is the "that was there the entire time."**

**What is the Builder life among the records?** His own. Dan reads a non-human memoir in the middle of the year, sympathetically, as the life of a dead Builder from before the end. In the last third he learns the writer is the figure standing at the bottom of the stair.

**What happened to the Linguist?** She stopped returning to the surface; the deep doors closed to her; she understood too late that the way down was by going out. She wrote her lessons for the next reader, learned REMAIN from the Custodian's own records, and used it on herself so that she would be there to see how it ended. She is in the walls of the last camp. Her final fragment, written in the Cut, holds both words and her uncertainty.

**What is the answer, and what does the player do?** At the Seed-place, the last door needs the largest Key and a word. The Custodian is there. He asks, once, without force, for the answering word. Dan cuts the binding word, KEEP-ALL, prepared by the Builders and never cut. The Seed goes quiet for good: nothing cut in the Site will ever carry again. Everything inside remains readable; every word Dan learned still works there. The Custodian, bound to the place by REMAIN, is bound quiet with it. He does not fight. In the walls of the Seed-place, after, in his own hand, is his last record: not a curse but a list, the names of his people, and at the end of the list, the Salt-Cutter's, the Surveyor's, the Copyist's, the Engineer's, the Linguist's, and a ring left empty.

**The coda.** The Linguist's first lesson-wall, the one that taught Dan the word for light in week two, re-read with the full script. Its last line, unreadable for a year: *"He lit it for me. I have left it lit for you. Read all of it. Then decide."*

## 7. The Custodian (summary; full treatment in `CHARACTERS.md`)

- **What he is:** a Builder: tall, pale, long-fingered, eyes large and nearly useless, reading by touch; he does not eat; his voice is low and exact; over ages REMAIN has made his lower body and one hand indistinguishable from the wall he stands against. Imposing by stillness and scale, never by threat.
- **What he does:** keeps. He lights the lamp. He teaches the first marks. He records lives. He cuts names. He never stops anyone, never lies, always omits.
- **What he wants:** to answer his dead and go to them.
- **His reason (comprehensible):** he built the Quiet and, by building it, was the only one kept out of his people's end. He has been alone longer than there have been humans. He believes his own safeguard exiled him from the only thing he ever wanted.
- **His error:** he thinks his people are in the Listeners. They are in the walls, in his own hand, and have been the whole time.
- **Staircase of revelation:** effect (month 1) → presence (month 2) → face (month 6) → reason (months 8–9) → nature (months 11–12).

## 8. Factions and minds (full treatment in `FACTIONS.md`)

- **The binders** (Builders, majority): keep it quiet; a school and a warning. Their work is the Site.
- **The answerers** (Builders, minority): answer and be joined. Their thesis survives in the Custodian.
- **The readers**: not a faction; a lineage of accident. Each was taught; each carried a piece; each stopped going up.
- **Human institutions of each age** appear only as pressure on a life: the road-works and its overseer; the copying house; the railway company; the institute that did not believe the Linguist. They never enter the Site.
- **The Speakers and the Listeners** are not factions: one is a gift, the other a process.

## 10. The ending (fixed)

Written first; nothing above or in any other sealed file may contradict it.

1. **Month 10–11.** Dan reaches the Seed-place through the last counting door (the largest Key, from a great real milestone, plus the word PATH-OPEN cut with the intensifier: the only time Dan is asked to be loud, and the Custodian's last help).
2. **The Seed.** A chamber of fused glassy stone, cut so densely it looks smooth. Every sign Dan knows is there. The two prepared words are cut beside a blank space at the centre: KEEP-ALL (the binders' word, never sounded) and VOICE-GIVE-FAR (the answerers' word, never sounded). The Custodian stands where the wall becomes him.
3. **He asks once.** One speech, late, no mechanism: what he believes, what he built, what it cost, what he has waited for. He does not threaten. He has never stopped anyone. He does not now.
4. **Dan cuts KEEP-ALL.** The ritual is the same one he has done for a year: mark, mark, the word locks, the place answers. The Seed goes quiet. Every lamp in the Site stays lit.
5. **What it costs.** The Custodian is kept quiet with the place. He is not destroyed; he becomes what he made. His last record appears in the Seed-place wall: the list of names, ending with the readers, and one empty ring.
6. **What Dan keeps.** Every word, every record, every route. The Site is his to read. Nothing in it will ever carry. The lamp is lit.
7. **The coda.** The lesson-wall re-read: *"He lit it for me. I have left it lit for you. Read all of it. Then decide."* The last line of the story is the first thing Dan ever read.

Why this ending: it is latent in the premise (a place built to finish a binding must end with the binding cut); it is the protagonist's alternative answer to a question the antagonist posed correctly; it is a re-reading of the whole year (the names, the lesson, the lamp); it costs something true; it is dark and kind at once; and it is complete, because Dan stops when the story ends.

## 11. Things that stay uncertain on purpose

- The nature of the Listeners (three theories; never settled on the page).
- What the Speakers looked like, or whether "looked" applies.
- Whether the Custodian, quiet in the wall, is aware. The Linguist's last line ("I do not know if he is right") stands for this too.
- Exactly what happened to the Surveyor in the deep (his crew's account and the Custodian's record disagree; both are honest).

## 12. Things that are settled and must not drift

- The Custodian never lies and never stops anyone. He omits.
- The records were cut by him except where stated.
- The name-rings are the Builders' names, cut by him.
- KEEP is the mid-point sign (month 5–7); it re-reads his name, the lintels and the lamp.
- The deep doors count returns; he cannot pass them; the Linguist could not either once she stopped going up.
- The Listeners' nature is not confirmed; the Custodian's hope is false in the author's truth.
- The ending is the cutting of KEEP-ALL; the Custodian does not fight; his last record is the list of names.
- The coda is the lesson-wall's last line.
