## 1. The Custodian

**Names.** In the Cut, a ring around KEEP-ONE: *the one who keeps*. The Salt-Cutter: "the tall one who does not eat." The Surveyor: "the Guest." The Copyist (who never met him): "the angel who closed the hill." The Engineer: "the Inspector." The Linguist: "the Custodian" (her translation of his ring; the sealed docs adopt it). Dan learns each name in that order and reads the ring in month 6.

**What he is.** The last Builder; the chief binder; the architect of the Quiet. Tall (the Salt-Cutter counts him at "two of me and a lamb"), pale as the inside of a shell, long-fingered, four fingers to a hand, eyes large and nearly useless, reading by touch with the flat of his fingers and by sound. He does not eat. His voice is low and exact and comes from lower in the chest than a man's. Over ages the word REMAIN has made him part of what he keeps: his feet and lower legs are the floor; his left hand, which he laid on the wall when he cut the word, is the wall; he stands where the corridor turns, and readers have taken him for a pillar until he spoke. He can move, slowly, along the laced stone, and only inside the Site. He cannot leave. He cannot pass a counting door.

**Presence (what makes him imposing).** Stillness and scale, never threat. He is always where you are going, not where you have been. He knows every word; he has cut most of what you read. He speaks your language, learned from the last reader, so the Linguist heard him in the Engineer's century-old English, and Dan (if he ever heard him) would hear hers. He is courteous. He asks permission to record you. He never raises his voice: he has spent an age building a place where no one has to.

**What he does.** Keeps. He lights the lamp for each new reader. He teaches the first marks, always the same ones, always by the same lesson (the lamp and the fire), because it worked the first time. He gives a rod. He records each life, in the reader's own words, with his formula at the top. He cuts his people's names into the walls, one ring at a time, and has not finished. He counts. He waits.

**What he wants.** To answer, and to go to them. He believes his people were not ended but taken up; that the Listeners are a gathering mind and to be found is to be joined; that the surface has grown louder every age and will be found regardless; and that the only dignified thing left is to be found *on purpose, whole, together*, by cutting VOICE-GIVE-FAR at the Seed. He cannot do it himself: the counting doors he designed exclude anyone who does not return from the world. So he needs a reader who reaches the bottom, and he has been improving the school for that reader for longer than there have been people.

**His reason (the one a sane person could hold).** He built the safeguard that kept him out of his people's end. He has been alone longer than there have been humans. Every reader he has taught has stopped going up and been shut out by his own doors. He is right that something takes; right that the language carries; right that readers have drawn it before. He is wrong that anything persists in it, and wrong that the surface's noise is the same as the Cut. He cannot afford to be wrong: if the Builders were right, his people are simply gone, and he has spent an age keeping a house for no one.

**His secret acts.**
- He shut the Surveyor's door. The Surveyor cut MOVE-STONE loud in a side gallery, and the water in the works' channel stood still. A small, uncontrolled carrying draws a piece of the process to one man; it does not bring "joining". To protect the *work* (a whole answer at the Seed), he shut the door on a living man. He records it honestly: "I shut the door. He long-slept not when I shut it." He does not excuse it. It is the darkest thing in the story and it is consistent with everything he believes.
- He let the Linguist remain. She asked. He wanted a witness as much as she wanted to see. He also knew that, once she had remained, the doors would never open for her; he told her that. She did it anyway.
- He allowed the lesson-wall, the rod and the lamp for the next reader. He would have done it himself; she did it better.

**His rule (fairness).** He never lies. He omits. Ask him a direct question and he will answer it truly or say that he will not answer. He never stops a reader reading. He has stopped exactly one reader *carrying*, once, and it cost the reader's life.

**Belief vs truth.**
| He believes | Truth |
|---|---|
| His people are in the Listeners | They are in the walls, in his own hand |
| The Listeners are a mind that keeps | A process that takes; nothing persists |
| The surface will be found anyway | Only the Cut carries |
| A reader who reaches the Seed will answer | The reader keeps |

**Staircase (RESEARCH lesson 30).** Effect (month 1: the lit lamp; the fresh cut; the sealed door): presence (month 2: a hand seen on the Stair; his hand-mark on every record): face (month 6: he stands at the Landing and speaks one sentence): reason (months 8–9: the memoir is his; the Surveyor's door; his thesis, in his own words, in the Linguist's notes): nature (months 11–12: the names; the list; the empty ring).

**His one speech (month 12).** Late, no mechanism, and it is *asked*, not demanded. What he built. What it cost. What he has waited for. What he believes is on the other side of the word. He says he will not stop Dan whichever word he cuts, and he does not. Written in full in `LIVES.md` §7.

**What happens to him.** Bound quiet with the place. Not destroyed; kept. His last record: the list.

## 2. Reader ONE — the Salt-Cutter

- **Station:** cuts rock salt from the hill's outcrop; sells it by the block; a widower with a daughter who keeps the tally-stick.
- **Form of record:** a tally-song, as transcribed: "I say what I saw." He counts everything (blocks, sheep, days, the tall one's fingers). Never says "I think". Plainest English of all the lives.
- **Desire:** to get home to his daughter before dark.
- **Fear:** that the thing in the hill is owed something (hospitality logic: he offers bread and salt; the tall one does not eat; he decides it is "a thing that is owed nothing and asks nothing," and is only partly right).
- **What he learned:** LAMP, FIRE, and LIGHT, cut with the tall one's hand over his. The hall woke. He wept, not from fear.
- **What he did:** asked to leave; was asked for the lamp ("someone will need it"); left it; went home; sealed the crack with stones and salt; told only his daughter; lived to be old and counted the tall one's fingers when he could not sleep.
- **His piece:** the lamp; the first word; the first description ("four on the hand, and long; two of me and a lamb"); the count in fives that the Custodian converts to eights (a clue, week 8).
- **Bias:** he treats the tall one as weather: neither good nor bad, just there. This is the *kindest* reading of the Custodian in the records, and it is the earliest.
- **Belief vs truth:** he thinks the tall one *lives* in the hill the way an owl lives in a barn. True enough.

## 3. Reader TWO — the Surveyor

- **Station:** engineer of the road-works; ambitious, precise, vain about his levels; kind to his crew and careful to get them paid.
- **Form:** "Report to the overseer of the works." Never "I"; "this surveyor". Tens and paces.
- **Desire:** to be the man who put a road through the hill. Then: to be the man who found what was under it.
- **Fear:** of being ordinary. (He is not afraid of the Guest; that is his flaw.)
- **What he learned:** LIGHT, OPEN-WAY, MOVE-STONE. A rod. He is the first to cut marks in his own hand, so two of his records are not transcriptions.
- **What he did:** treated the Site as his find and the Custodian as his *guest*. Took his crew in. Went deeper than the crew would follow. Cut MOVE-STONE loud in a side gallery to reach a lower level ("a great cut"). The channel's water stood still; the mules would not enter. The Custodian shut the gallery door with him inside.
- **His last record** (own hand, in the shut chamber, found month 8–9): "Report to the overseer of the works. This surveyor has measured the chamber. Eleven paces by seven. The door does not answer to the word. The Guest was right about the water. The men are owed for nine days; their names follow." Then the names. No accusation. That is the dark room, and the kind line inside it.
- **His piece:** the rod (in the chamber with him); the measuring cord (dropped on the Stair); the first *loud* use and the first anomaly; the first record in a reader's own hand.
- **Belief vs truth:** he believed the hill was his. The crew believed the Guest closed the hill on purpose. The Custodian's record agrees with the crew.

## 4. Reader THREE — the Copyist

- **Station:** a woman in a copying house; copies old engineering collections for a patron; solitary; devout and sceptical at once; funny about ink.
- **Form:** "In the margin:" Leaves, quires, hours of the office. Never "we".
- **Desire:** to copy without error. Then: to understand the marks she is copying.
- **Fear:** of the blot; of being moved to the kitchens.
- **What she learned:** by attention alone, the negation rule ("the little cross comes after the word it undoes; I have seen it eleven times and it never comes before"). She thought it a rule of an angelic alphabet. She was right about the rule.
- **What she did:** illuminated the marks in gold in the margins of the Surveyor's report; argued with the report in the margins ("the Guest was an angel who closed the hill to keep men out; the surveyor was proud"); preserved the only copy of the account.
- **Her piece:** the book (carried in by the Engineer; in the Loud Room); the negation rule (Dan learns NOT in week 9 partly through her margin); the first *theory* of the Custodian; the reverent misreading (RESEARCH lesson 13: the player ahead of the record).
- **Belief vs truth:** she believed the Guest protected men. He shut a man in. She never knew. The Custodian counts her as a reader "who never came", and the Linguist finds that the saddest line in the Site.

## 5. Reader FOUR — the Engineer

- **Station:** a railway company's tunnelling engineer; practical, dry, a reader; borrowed the Copyist's book from the regional library because the locals said the hill "is written".
- **Form:** "Log." Feet, yards, pounds of powder, shifts, the day of the month and never the year. Never "afraid".
- **Desire:** to drive the tunnel through on schedule. Then: to make the hill answer him.
- **Fear:** (never written) that the sound in the sky was for him.
- **What he learned:** LIGHT, OPEN-WAY, MOVE-STONE; KEEP, which he read as "hold" (a mining word: hold the roof) and used as HOLD; LOUD, shown to him as a warning on a lintel.
- **What he did:** took the Inspector for a company man in the dark ("a tall man with a lamp asked what I was cutting; I told him a railway"). Cut MOVE-STONE with LOUD, with powder, to bring down a whole gallery at once: the loudest use in human ages. The village well stood still three days; compasses turned to the hill; the sound. His crew fled. The company shut the works. He stayed; took his meals down; stopped going up. The doors below closed to him. He believed the sound was the hill answering and that louder would open it. Died of a fall in the Loud Room. The Custodian laid him in the Hold.
- **His piece:** the Loud Room (ECHO plays the blast); the book; his stopped watch; the well-keeper's and the company's accounts (chorus); the clearest case of *loud use → anomaly*; the wrong-but-working reading of KEEP as HOLD.
- **Belief vs truth:** he believed the hill answered him. Something answered; not the hill.

## 6. Reader FIVE — the Linguist

- **Station:** a scholar of dead languages, sidelined by her institute for pursuing an "engineer's ghost story"; alone by temperament; exact; funny.
- **Form:** "Note." Metres, days since descent. Present tense; no ONCE. Never "he says" without "I think" (she is scrupulous about not treating his claims as fact: she is the story's model of rule 3).
- **Desire:** at first, to decipher. Then, to be answered. Then, to know how it ends.
- **Grief (the accomplice's comprehensible grief, RESEARCH lesson 33):** she had spent her life reading people who could not hear her. The Cut was the first dead language that answered back. She and the Custodian recognised each other: the two loneliest readers in the world.
- **What she learned:** some thirty signs; the grammar (act first; negation after; give = answer); the memoir; the safeguard; REMAIN.
- **What she did:** reopened the shaft; met him in the first hall; named him from his ring; talked with him for months; argued ("I do not know if he is right"); stopped going up around her fortieth day; read the RETURN marks on the counting doors too late; carried the lamp back to the first hall; made the lesson-wall for the next reader; asked him to keep the lamp lit; transcribed her dead tapes onto stone; learned REMAIN from his workshop and used it to stay and see.
- **Her piece:** the lesson-wall (the first word); the rod; the notes (her hand, present tense, the age-marker misdirection); the grammar; the theory that the Listeners are a process ("no one is in it"); the discovery that the memoir is his (her note in the last camp); the two words copied out side by side; the last line of the story.
- **Belief vs truth:** she half-believed him. She wrote both words down and did not choose. She is in the walls of her last camp, and the Custodian's list ends with her ring and then an empty one.
- **Voice sample:** "Note. Day 31. He speaks English like a railway timetable from a hundred and fifty years ago. I asked where he learned it. He said: from the fourth. I have started to think of the Engineer as a colleague."

## 7. The Builders (as a people; the memoir is the Custodian's)

- **Form:** "We cut." First person plural throughout; "I" appears in one fragment only, the last.
- **How they thought:** as a *we*. Touch and sound. Patience over centuries. Light was the first word and the least useful to them; they made it because the seed's first lesson was light, and they felt the warmth on the wall before they could see.
- **The split:** binders ("we who keep") and answerers ("we who send"). The answerers' argument survives on one wall of the Council Gallery, in another hand: "What is kept is buried. What is sent is heard. We were sent. We are the proof."
- **The safeguard, in their words:** "We made the deep doors to open only to one who keeps leaving. The one who stays cannot go down. We made them against ourselves, against what we might become."
- **The end:** "Each cutting, fewer in the count. Those who went quiet did not long-sleep. They were not there."
- **The last fragment:** "I am in the deep. I cut this. There is no we."

## 8. The Speakers

Not a character. A voice with no one behind it (RESEARCH lesson 14): the seed's own text, found in the deep and at the Seed-place, which describes the language in the language ("We divide the world thus: what is put out; what is taken in; what is kept") and folds the warning at its end. Dan learns to mark these records as *no-hand* (they carry no hand-mark, the only records that don't) in month 7–8.
