## 1. The Custodian

**Names.** In the Cut, a ring around KEEP-ONE: *the one who keeps*. The Salt-Cutter: "the tall one who does not eat." The Surveyor: "the Guest." The Copyist (who never met him): "the angel who closed the hill." The Engineer: "the Inspector." The Linguist: "the Tenant" in her notebook (dry, wrong by inversion), then, in her cut notes, his ring itself: keep-one, "not a name; what he does" (L15a). "The Custodian" is the sealed docs' name for that reading; the app never shows it. Dan meets the names in this order: the tall one (week 2), the Tenant (week 5), the Guest (week 11, C1; the Cut says only "one other"), the Inspector and the angel (week 11), keep-one (month 6, when he reads the ring).

**What he is.** The last Builder; the chief binder; the architect of the Quiet. Tall (the Salt-Cutter counts him at "two of me and a lamb"), pale as the inside of a shell, long-fingered, four fingers to a hand, eyes large and nearly useless, reading by touch with the flat of his fingers and by sound. He does not eat. His voice is low and exact and comes from lower in the chest than a man's. Over ages the word REMAIN has made him of the stone: his feet are floor; when he stands still long enough the wall takes his outline (the print of his left hand is beside REMAIN in his workshop, in the stone to the wrist); he stands where the corridor turns, and readers have taken him for a pillar until he spoke. He moves slowly, along laced passages only, inside the Site, in the halls above the three counting doors. He cannot leave. He cannot pass a counting door, and he has not been below them since his people ended (WORLD_TRUTH rule 6). He learns languages by listening through the rock: he had the salt-cutters' tongue before the first of them came in, and each reader's tongue from the one before.

**Presence (what makes him imposing).** Stillness and scale, never threat. The floor at the first hall's corner is worn into two troughs a stride apart and the wall polished to the shoulder height of a man twice Dan's size: the Site vouches for him in week one before anyone describes him. He is always further in, until the doors. He knows every word; he has cut most of what you read. He speaks your language, learned from the last reader, so the Linguist heard him in the Engineer's English, a railway age old, and Dan, when he hears him, hears hers. He is courteous. He asks permission to record you. He never raises his voice: he has spent an age building a place where no one has to. He has one kind of wit, dry and exact, and uses it perhaps twice a year.

**What he does.** Keeps. He lights the lamp for each new reader. He teaches the first marks, always the same ones, always by the same lesson (the lamp and the fire), because it worked the first time. He gives a rod. He records each life, in the reader's own words, with his formula at the top. He cuts his people's names into the walls, one ring at a time, and has not finished. He counts.

**What he wants.** To answer, and to go to them. He believes his people were not ended but taken up; that the Listeners are a gathering mind and to be found is to be joined; that the surface has grown louder every age and will be found regardless; and that the only dignified thing left is to be found *on purpose, whole, together*, by cutting VOICE-GIVE-FAR at the Seed. He cannot do it himself: the counting doors he designed exclude anyone who does not return from the world, and the Seed's words lock only under a counted hand. So he needs a reader who reaches the bottom, and he has been improving the school for that reader for longer than there have been people. He has never read the Seed's finest cut: no Builder could; it is read by light.

**The readers' personal names** (known to Dan only from the list, month 12; all year they are known by trade). Salt-Cutter: **Hamun**; his wife **Ashti**; his daughter **Nira**. Surveyor: **Sertor**. Copyist: **Alditha**. Engineer: **Fenwick** (his log is signed so). Linguist: **Ines Halloran**. These are texture, not dating: no country is implied by any of them and the app never shows them before the list.

**His reason (the one a sane person could hold).** He built the safeguard that kept him out of his people's end. He has been alone longer than there have been humans. Every reader but the first stopped going up, and his own doors opened no further for them. He is right that something takes; right that the language carries; right that readers have drawn it before. He is wrong that anything persists in it, and wrong that the surface's noise is the same as the Cut. He cannot afford to be wrong: if the Builders were right, his people are simply gone, and he has spent an age keeping a house for no one.

**His one test, and his wound.** In the great age he cut LOUD himself, once, in a side hall, and the rock went still under his hand (B4's "three times" are the Builders'; the fourth was his). He knows what a small carrying does: it comes for one man or one gallery and lets go. That is why he never tried his cure alone: a whole answer needs the Seed and a counted hand. And he went to the mouth once, before REMAIN, to see whether the count would take a maker: the light shut his eyes and the count was one. Builders were not made for the sun; that was the point of the doors, and it is his own exclusion. He told the first reader to go, and the first came back only when he was old; he read that as losing a reader for a lifetime and never said it to anyone again, which is why every later reader stopped going up and why the doors opened no further for them. The one instruction that would have given him what he wanted is the one he could not bear to give.

**His secret acts.**
- He shut the Surveyor's door. The Surveyor cut MOVE-STONE loud in a side gallery, was warned, waited nine days, and cut it loud a second time; the water in the works' channel stood still and has not moved since. A small, uncontrolled carrying draws a piece of the process to one man; it does not bring "joining". To protect the *work* (a whole answer at the Seed), he shut the door on a living man. He records it honestly: "I shut the door. He long-slept not when I shut it." He does not excuse it. It is the darkest thing in the story and it is consistent with everything he believes.
- He did not shut the Engineer's. The Surveyor was deep in the galleries, a few halls above the Builders' halls and a straight way from them; the Engineer was in an outer chamber the Custodian could close off afterwards (K3, cut when he laid him in the Hold). And by the Engineer's age he had decided that a loud reader was worth more to him than a quiet one: he showed him the LOUD sign as a warning, and let him.
- He let the Linguist remain. She asked. He did not refuse her. He also knew that, once she had remained, the doors would open no further for her; he told her that. She did it anyway.
- He allowed the lesson-wall, the rod and the lamp for the next reader. He would have done it himself; she did it better.

**His rule (fairness).** He never lies. He omits. Ask him a direct question and he will answer it truly or say that he will not answer. He never stops a reader reading. He has stopped exactly one reader *carrying*, once, and it cost the reader's life.

**Belief vs truth.**
| He believes | Truth |
|---|---|
| His people are in the Listeners | They are in the walls, in his own hand |
| The Listeners are a mind that keeps | A process that takes; nothing persists |
| The surface will be found anyway | Only the Cut carries |
| A reader who reaches the Seed will answer | The reader keeps |

**Staircase (RESEARCH lesson 30).** Effect (month 1: the lit lamp; the worn corner; the sharp ring among smooth ones): presence (month 2: a hand seen on the Stair; his hand-mark on every record): face (month 6: he stands at the Landing and speaks one sentence): reason (months 8–9: the memoir is his; the Surveyor's door; his thesis, in his own words, in the Linguist's notes): nature (months 11–12: the names; the list; the empty ring).

**His one speech (month 12).** Late, no mechanism, and it is *asked*, not demanded; it comes down the lacing from the three doors, because he cannot stand where Dan stands. No hedging. The tie-break ("we were even; I was the one more"); the mouth ("the count was one"); the horror ("all the names at once. Yours too. I have no name to send you under"); why he never told anyone to go out; why he will not ask for a loud return (it came for the second); the stake, said aloud (the keeping-word silences him with the place); and the sixth reader's record, which is only the count so far. He asks once. He does not stop Dan. Written in full in `LIVES.md` §7.

**What happens to him.** Bound quiet with the place. Not destroyed; kept. The wall at the doors takes his outline. His last record, on the door's face: the list, headed by his own ring (a job, not a name), ending with the readers' own names and an empty ring for the sixth reader, whose name he was never given.

## 2. Reader ONE — the Salt-Cutter

- **Station:** cuts rock salt from the hill's outcrop; sells it by the block; a widower with a daughter who keeps the tally-stick.
- **Form of record:** a tally-song, as transcribed: "I say what I saw." He counts everything (blocks, sheep, days, the tall one's fingers). Never says "I think". Plainest English of all the lives.
- **Desire:** to get home to his daughter before dark.
- **Fear:** that the thing in the hill is owed something (hospitality logic: he offers bread and salt; the tall one does not eat; he decides it is "a thing that is owed nothing and asks nothing," and is only partly right).
- **What he learned:** LAMP, FIRE, and LIGHT, cut with the tall one's hand over his. The hall woke. He wept, not from fear.
- **What he did:** asked to leave; was asked for the lamp ("someone will need it"); left it; went home; sealed the crack with stones and salt; told only his daughter; lived to be old and counted the tall one's fingers when he could not sleep. In old age he opened the crack once more, went in with a new lamp, found his old lamp lit, told his days to the tall one to be cut, and sealed the crack again. He is the only reader who went home, and the only one who came back.
- **His piece:** the lamp; the first word; the first description ("four on the hand, and long; two of me and a lamb"); the count in fives that the Custodian converts to eights (a clue, week 8).
- **Bias:** he treats the tall one as weather: neither good nor bad, just there. This is the *kindest* reading of the Custodian in the records, and it is the earliest.
- **Belief vs truth:** he thinks the tall one *lives* in the hill the way an owl lives in a barn. True enough.

## 3. Reader TWO — the Surveyor

- **Station:** engineer of the road-works; ambitious, precise, vain about his levels; kind to his crew and careful to get them paid.
- **Form:** "Report to the overseer of the works." Never "I"; "this surveyor". Tens and paces.
- **Desire:** to be the man who put a road through the hill. Then: to be the man who found what was under it.
- **Fear:** of being ordinary. (He is not afraid of the Guest; that is his flaw.)
- **What he learned:** LIGHT, OPEN-WAY, MOVE-STONE. A rod. He is the first to cut marks in his own hand, so two of his records are not transcriptions.
- **What he did:** treated the Site as his find and the Custodian as his *guest*. Took his crew in. Went deeper than the crew would follow. Cut MOVE-STONE loud in a side gallery to reach a lower level ("a great cut"). The channel's water stood still; the mules would not enter. The Custodian shut the gallery door with him inside.
- **His last record** (own hand, in the shut chamber, found month 8–9): "Report to the overseer of the works. This surveyor has measured the chamber. Eleven paces by seven. The door does not answer to the word. The Guest was right about the water. The men are owed for thirteen days; their names follow." Then the names. No accusation. That is the dark room, and the kind line inside it.
- **His piece:** the rod (in the chamber with him); the measuring cord (dropped on the Stair); the first *loud* use and the first anomaly; the first record in a reader's own hand.
- **Belief vs truth:** he believed the hill was his. The crew believed the Guest closed the hill on purpose. The Custodian's record agrees with the crew.

## 4. Reader THREE — the Copyist

- **Station:** a woman in a copying house; copies old engineering collections for a patron; solitary; devout and sceptical at once; funny about ink.
- **Form:** "In the margin:" Leaves, quires, hours of the office. Never "we".
- **Desire:** to copy without error. Then: to understand the marks she is copying.
- **Fear:** of the blot; of being moved to the kitchens.
- **What she learned:** by attention alone, the negation rule ("the little cross comes after the word it undoes; I have seen it eleven times and it never comes before"). She thought it a rule of an angelic alphabet. She was right about the rule. She also glossed the fork set over a sign (LOUD) as *a rank of angel*, which is how the Engineer, who learned his marks from her book, came to read the warning on a lintel as a rating. The reader who never came is load-bearing on the loudest act in human ages.
- **What she did:** illuminated the marks in gold in the margins of the Surveyor's report; argued with the report in the margins ("the Guest was an angel who closed the hill to keep men out; the surveyor was proud"); preserved the only copy of the account.
- **Her piece:** the book (carried in by the Engineer; in the Loud Room); the negation rule (Dan learns NOT in week 9 partly through her margin); the first *theory* of the Custodian; the reverent misreading (RESEARCH lesson 13: the player ahead of the record).
- **Belief vs truth:** she believed the Guest protected men. He shut a man in. She never knew. The Custodian counts her as a reader "who never came", and the Linguist finds that the saddest line in the Site.

## 5. Reader FOUR — the Engineer

- **Station:** a railway company's tunnelling engineer; practical, dry, a reader; borrowed the Copyist's book from the regional library because the locals said the hill "is written".
- **Form:** "Log." Feet, yards, pounds of powder, shifts, the day of the month and never the year. Never "afraid".
- **Desire:** to drive the tunnel through on schedule. Then: to make the hill answer him.
- **Fear:** (never written) that the sound in the sky was for him.
- **What he learned:** LIGHT, OPEN-WAY, MOVE-STONE; KEEP, which he read as "hold" (a mining word: hold the roof) and used as HOLD; LOUD, shown to him as a warning on a lintel.
- **What he did:** took the Inspector for a company man in the dark ("a tall man with a lamp asked what I was cutting; I told him a railway"). Cut MOVE-STONE with LOUD, with powder, to bring down a whole gallery at once: the loudest use in human ages. The village well stood still three days; compasses turned to the hill; the sound. His crew fled. The company shut the works. He stayed; took his meals down; stopped going up. The doors below opened no further for him. He believed the sound was the hill answering and that louder would open it. Died of a fall in the Loud Room. The Custodian laid him in the Hold.
- **His piece:** the Loud Room (ECHO plays the blast); the book; his stopped watch; the well-keeper's and the company's accounts (chorus); the clearest case of *loud use → anomaly*; the wrong-but-working reading of KEEP as HOLD.
- **Belief vs truth:** he believed the hill answered him. Something answered; not the hill.

## 6. Reader FIVE — the Linguist

- **Station:** a scholar of dead languages, sidelined by her institute for pursuing an "engineer's ghost story"; alone by temperament; exact; funny.
- **Form:** "Note." Metres, days since descent. Present tense; no ONCE. Never "he says" without "I think" (she is scrupulous about not treating his claims as fact: she is the story's model of rule 3).
- **Desire:** at first, to decipher. Then, to be answered. Then, to read all of it.
- **Grief (the accomplice's comprehensible grief, RESEARCH lesson 33):** she had spent her life reading people who could not hear her. The Cut was the first dead language that answered back. She and the Custodian recognised each other: the two loneliest readers in the world.
- **What she learned:** sixty-odd signs, more than Dan holds in a year (she needed them for the memoir and both words); the grammar (act first; negation after; give = answer); the memoir; the safeguard; REMAIN. She went down at Day 40 with everything her car held; her cut notes span roughly Days 40–140.
- **What she did:** reopened the shaft; met him in the first hall; called him "the Tenant" in her notebook (dry, and wrong: he is the landlord) until she read his ring in her cut notes and corrected herself (keep-one: not a name); made the lesson-wall for the next reader while she was still going up (around her twentieth day); talked with him for months; argued ("I do not know if he is right"); stopped going up around her fortieth day and went down past the three doors with the count she had; read the safeguard and chose; came back up once for the lamp, carried it to the first hall, went out to the field for an hour so the doors would let her down again, and asked him to keep the lamp lit; transcribed her dead tapes onto stone; copied REMAIN from his workshop wall before she went down, and cut it at the last door: "I would rather be a wall in this than a person anywhere else." Not a vigil; the Site shows no awareness in her wall.
- **Her piece:** the lesson-wall (the first word); the rod; the notes (her hand, present tense, the age-marker misdirection); the grammar; the theory that the Listeners are a process ("no one is in it"); the discovery that the memoir is his (her note in the last camp); the two words copied out side by side; the last line of the story.
- **Belief vs truth:** she half-believed him. She wrote both words down and did not choose. She is the wall of her last camp (no figure in it: a smooth face, the rod to the wrist, her hand-mark), and the Custodian's list ends with her name and then an empty ring for the one who came after her.
- **Voice sample:** "Note. Day 31. He speaks English like a railway timetable that nobody has updated since the railway. I asked where he learned it. He said: from the fourth. I have started to think of the Engineer as a colleague."

## 7. The Builders (as a people; the memoir is the Custodian's)

- **Form:** "We cut." First person plural throughout; "I" appears in one fragment only, the last. B1–B6 carry his Builder-age hand-mark (the mark he used before he took the name KEEP-ONE); B7 alone carries KEEP-ONE. The change of mark is the clue (C-55; a hand-mark, recognisable without NAME).
- **How they thought:** as a *we*. Touch and sound. Patience over centuries. Light was the first word and the least useful to them; they made it because the seed's first lesson was light, and they felt the warmth on the wall before they could see.
- **The split:** binders ("we who keep") and answerers ("we who send"). The answerers' argument survives on one wall of the Council Gallery, in another hand: "What is kept is buried. What is sent is heard. We were sent. We are the proof."
- **The safeguard, in their words:** "We made the deep doors to open only to one who keeps leaving. The one who stays cannot go down. We made them against ourselves, against what we might become."
- **The end:** "Each cutting, fewer in the count. Those who went quiet did not long-sleep. They were not there."
- **The last fragment:** "I am in the deep. I cut this. There is no we."

## 8. The Speakers

Not a character. A voice with no one behind it (RESEARCH lesson 14): the seed's own text, found in the deep and at the Seed-place, which describes the language in the language ("We divide the world thus: what is put out; what is taken in; what is kept") and folds the warning at its end. Dan learns to mark these records as *no-hand* (they carry no hand-mark, the only records that don't) in month 7–8.

## 10. The secondary lives in depth: how each thread turns the centre

The three shorter lives are not episodes. Each one turns the central story once, and each is the reason the next one exists.

### The Surveyor (reader TWO)
- **Desire, stated:** put a road through the hill. **Desire, real:** be the one who found what was under it. He writes "this surveyor" because he is already writing for posterity.
- **Secret (never in his reports):** he heard the Guest's warning, believed it, and cut anyway, because the road would not wait and because the water standing still *proved* the place was his to prove. His last report ("the Guest was right about the water") is the only place he admits it.
- **What he knows that Dan needs:** the first loud use; the first anomaly; the Custodian's warning *before* the act; the boy's tablet.
- **How his thread turns the centre:** (1) his loud cut is the first evidence that using the language *draws* something, planted months before LOUD is readable; (2) the shut door is the antagonist's one act of force, told honestly in the antagonist's own hand, which is what makes the Custodian's "never stopped anyone reading" a *distinction* rather than a lie; (3) his boy's tablet is how the marks left the hill: without him, no Copyist, no book, no Engineer, no Linguist. The chain of readers is his fault.
- **What the Custodian thinks of him:** the reader he failed. "I will not tell you it was right."

### The Copyist (reader THREE)
- **Desire, stated:** copy without error. **Desire, real:** to understand the one thing in the collection that was not a report.
- **Secret:** she believed the marks were holy and never told the patron what she thought she was gilding; she asked for more gold and lied about why ("because the words are long").
- **What she knows that Dan needs:** the negation rule, found by attention alone; the *misreading* of the fork-over-sign as a rank of angels; the theory that the Guest was a protector.
- **How her thread turns the centre:** (1) her rule teaches Dan NOT through the Linguist's report of it, the first re-reading; (2) her gold on the fork is why the Engineer, who learned his marks from her book, read the warning on the lintel as a rating and cut the loudest word in human ages: the reader who never came is load-bearing on the loudest act; (3) "he counts her anyway" is the first evidence that the keeper keeps *everyone*, and it lands in the Loud Room, the darkest room of the first quarter, as its kind line. Her blank page "in case" the surveyor's last report turned up is answered by V7, nine hundred years late, and Dan is the one who turns the page.
- **What the Custodian thinks of her:** the reader he never met and counted anyway. Her name is on the list because the Engineer said it once.

### The Engineer (reader FOUR)
- **Desire, stated:** through. **Desire, real:** to be answered. "The hill answered" is the sentence his whole decline hangs on.
- **Secret (the log never says "afraid"):** he knew the fork was a warning. "The Inspector had shown me the loud-mark on a lintel and said it was a warning. The book has it in gold as a rank. Took it for a rating." He chose the book's reading because it let him cut.
- **What he knows that Dan needs:** KEEP read as "hold", a wrong reading the place accepted (the language forgives readers; the Custodian corrects the word, not the man); the loudest use and its three anomalies; that the company's closure and trial shaft are why the Linguist could get in; the book, carried down.
- **How his thread turns the centre:** (1) his "hold" is why the Linguist's sheets say "held" over KEEP, which is why S4 reads "he held me" for nine weeks, which is why NOT and then KEEP turn it: the mid-point is built on his misreading; (2) his anomalies are the Linguist's table, and the table is the Custodian's proof; (3) he is the reader the Custodian *let* be loud, which is the first sign the antagonist's stance has changed since the Surveyor; (4) his English is the Custodian's English, and so the Custodian's speech is in the voice of a railway timetable.
- **What the Custodian thinks of him:** the reader he chose not to stop. He laid him in the Hold and kept the book.

### The chain, in one line
The Salt-Cutter leaves the lamp; the Surveyor's boy copies the marks; the Copyist gilds them and misreads one; the Engineer reads her gold and cuts loud; the company sinks a shaft; the Linguist reads his log and comes down; she makes a wall for Dan. Nothing in the chain was meant. Everything in it was kept.
