## 0. How reading works in the app (the three layers)

A record in the Cut is shown as its signs. Over them sit up to three layers:

1. **The app's voice** (plain, warm, P14) describes what is physically there: the place, the objects, the hand, the pictures. It never translates and never suggests an age.
2. **Her layer.** The Linguist left **loose glossary sheets, dated Days 1–15**, tucked into a crack beside the salt tally (the box sheet in her tin box): her first glosses of S1, S2 and S4 of the salt tally, the head of the Surveyor's report (V1) and V2's line about the crew, made before she learned the cross and never revised because she moved to cutting in stone. Where a sheet glosses a span, that gloss shows in her hand, in italics. Her glosses are honest and *sometimes wrong* (she gave the contested sign no word on her Day 1 and Day 4 sheets, only a question mark under it on the box sheet, and "gave" on the Day 9 sheet; she read KEEP as "held" because the Engineer's log had it as "hold", so "he held me" where the unlearned cross turns it). Her layer is the bilingual that bootstraps week 1 (RESEARCH lesson 16), and the first span Dan reads past her is the first thing that is his.
3. **Dan's layer.** Every sign he has learned renders as its English in every record that contains it; his guesses show with a question mark; confirmed signs settle. Where his layer and hers disagree, both show, and the difference is a beat. His NOT applies to spans she glossed: "he held me" becomes "he held me [not]" the week he learns the cross.

Between the layers, unheld signs stay as signs; carved pictures are described in brackets. So a fragment planted in week 1 can be *seen* in week 1, *half-read through her* in week 1, and *fully read by him* in month 3 or 4, and the three readings are three beats.

Her **notebook** (L2–L15) is English on paper, read a page at a time; it is not in the Cut. Her lesson-wall (L1), the rod's handle (L4) and everything she cut after Day 40 (L15a onward) are in the Cut.

## 0.1 The fragment rules

**A Cut record**: 8–24 signs from SCRIPT §3, plus numbers, rings, hand-marks and a few carved pictures (the Salt-Cutter's tally carries the most, because he counted in things); a terse rendering of 15–40 words in the act-first grammar; the formula at the head; one beat. **A telling** (author's reference; her expansions): 40–80 words, one witness by trade, one drawable object, one measure in the age's counting, one plain fact about the Site, one open question the writer couldn't answer, no address to the finder except gratitude. Draft the telling, then cut the record from it.

Prose test before any fragment ships: one drawable noun; no capitalised abstraction; no triad; no "whispers"; no wistful final cadence more than once a week; distinct voices by what each age counts and compares.

Notation below: `·` separates sign groups; `[ ]` is a carved picture; `ring(…)` is a name-ring; `{h}` a hand-mark. The formula `ONCE ring(KEEP-ONE) N` opens every transcription (his ring, the reader's number: "Once, [the one who keeps]: the first"), and the closing line `MAKE {h} VOICE OF N AGAIN / AGAIN NOT` ends it ("cut by [hand], from the voice of the first; came again"); renderings below omit the closing tag.

## 1. Reader ONE — the Salt-Cutter (S)

**Where:** cut along the Salt Gallery wall in the Custodian's hand, from the Salt-Cutter's own telling when he came back in old age (TIMELINE). Counts in hands (fives); the transcription writes his count and the transcriber's numeral in eights beside it (the double notation, visible week 8: in the strings below, `HAND | 5` is a hand-sign with a small row of five strokes cut beside it, `HAND HAND | 8+2` two hands with a bar and two strokes; ARR2 8.B shows the strokes, L9 says what they are). The numeral is cut beside the line, not in it, and the record view shows it beside, so a rendering's *hand* stays *hand*. Closing formula (S8 only): SEE ALL, "all I saw". Voice: he counts everything; never "I think"; plainest English; pictures of animals and salt.

## 7. The Custodian's one speech (month 12)

Spoken, in the Linguist's English, and heard at the Seed-place through the stone: he stands at the three doors, the furthest he can come, and the lacing carries his voice down. He speaks the way she heard him, like a timetable: act first, units, metaphor only from stone and count. No hedging, no reproach: he has believed one thing for an age. The app renders it as speech, once, in full (about 440 words, delivered a paragraph at a time: a phone's worth at a timetable's pace). He is asked nothing; he speaks because Dan has come, and he asks once. He cannot hear back (sound carries inward and down, never out; the app says so once, after the speech: *Nothing you say here goes up*): the ask is spoken into a place from which no answer returns. Every sentence is true, and the stake is said aloud: the keeping-word silences him with the place. The ask is coherent: the answering word, cut in the quiet, locks and waits in the stone, and a louder age may sound it; he asks for the waiting, not the sending; the keeping-word forecloses that, which is what the binding costs him. Nothing in the speech restates what the Cut has already shown Dan (the quietening, the doors opening no further, the lamp's history): those are in B6–B7, L16 and S4–L20 and stay there.

> You kept coming back. I cut the doors to want that. Then I could not do it myself. That is my life, if you want it short.
>
> We were glad, so we were loud. Then we read the last of the stone we could read, and it said what you have read. Half of us said keep. Half said send. We were even. I was the one more. I drew the doors so that nothing that lived only in the dark could open the bottom. I went to the mouth once, before the word, to see if the count would take a maker. The light shut my eyes and the count was one. I did not know what we would become. It was me.
>
> When I came up there was no one to keep.
>
> They are not ended. They are taken up, and kept, and the fifth's table is the times it reached for us and closed on one man, or one gallery, and let go. Your surface is louder every age; I hear it through the rock: wheels, then wires, then the sky. It will be found. The only thing left to cut is whether we go to it whole, all the names at once, on purpose. Yours too. I have no name to send you under. I did not ask your days. The count is all I have of you.
>
> I told the first to go, and he went, and he came back when he was old. I did not say it again to anyone. That is why the others stopped. I shut a door on the second. I will not tell you it was right. I have kept a lamp lit since the first, because it is the first word, and because it is dark.
>
> I began your record. It is only the count so far. Returned. Returned. Returned. It has no closing line. Every other one has. You keep going out.
>
> Two words. I cannot lock either, and I cannot stand where you stand. I ask you, once, for the answering word. It will not carry in there; you cut the quiet to come in. Cut, it waits, and a louder age may sound it. I ask for the waiting, not the sending. To carry it now you would come back loud through my halls, and a loud word in a hall brings it to one man. It came for the second. I will not do that twice. If you cut the keeping-word, I go quiet with it. I drew the word; I know what it is for. I will be at the doors.

If he cuts nothing, nothing changes and nothing nudges (WORLD_TRUTH §10). On the way up (week 51, its own visit), the list (K4) is on the door's face, and the wall beside it has taken his outline.

**His one other line of the year (the Landing, month 6):** "You have kept coming back. Good. Most of them stopped."
