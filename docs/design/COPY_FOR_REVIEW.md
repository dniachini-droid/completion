## Brief for the reviewer (ChatGPT, please read all of this first)

### What this is

A single-player fantasy/sci-fi RPG on an iPhone, made for one player (Dan). Real life drives it: when Dan works on a real job (ordering his cat's medication, studying Spanish, sorting the post), the expedition in the game travels deeper underground. A timed work session is called a **delve**. As delve-minutes add up, the expedition walks on through a vast, ancient, lamp-lit underground place of cut stone, water and falling light. When Dan finishes his day's jobs, he **arrives** somewhere: either a named chamber (with a full-screen painting of it) or, if he hasn't reached one yet, a **camp** for the night along the way.

The art style is painterly: warm lamp-gold against deep violet-blue dark, cut stone, haze, light falling from far above. Quiet, ancient, awed, not threatening. Think of the hush of a cathedral crypt crossed with the strangeness of a place built by no one you know.

### The player must not be spoiled

Dan is the player and will read your reply. So:

1. **Do not talk about the story.** No guesses about what the place is, who built it, what's at the bottom, what anything means or foreshadows. No commentary on "the mystery". Return the file, nothing else, except the short notes asked for below, which must be about *writing only*.
2. **Do not invent new facts about the world.** The game's story is already fixed in secret, and every clue must match a truth decided in advance. So describe only what is already implied in each current line: stone, stairs, water, light, dust, air, sound, temperature, scale, texture, age. **Do not add** people, creatures, bodies, names, writing, symbols, carvings, doors, machines, artefacts, colours of glowing things, or anything that sounds like a clue. If a current line already hints at something (e.g. "Someone swept this floor"), you may deepen the feeling of it but must not explain it or add to it.
3. **Do not name new places.** Place names stay exactly as they are.

### What Dan wants

He says the current wording "sounds like AI" and is too bare. He wants it to read like **real fantasy / science-fiction prose**: descriptive, specific, with a sense of actually being there, so that each room explains what is happening and what it feels like to stand in it. Not the same sentence reworded. Rewritten properly, by a writer.

What "sounds like AI" means here, so please avoid it:
- stock phrases: "a testament to", "whispers of", "ancient secrets", "echoes of the past", "tapestry", "a sense of", "palpable", "beckons", "delve into", "journey", "embark";
- triplets of adjectives, and every sentence the same length and rhythm;
- vague grandeur instead of a concrete detail ("the air is heavy with history" → no; "the steps are worn into shallow bowls in the middle" → yes);
- telling the reader how to feel.

Good models for the voice: Ursula K. Le Guin (*The Tombs of Atuan*), Gene Wolfe, M. John Harrison, Susanna Clarke (*Piranesi*, the closest match for tone), Jeff VanderMeer at his quietest. Present tense, second person or no person ("The stair goes down…", "You come out onto…"). British English.

### Hard rules for every line

- **No pressure, guilt or shame.** Never imply Dan failed, is behind, or owes anything. No streaks, no "don't give up", no "you missed". Stopping is always fine; rest is good.
- **No productivity-app voice.** No "Great job!", "You crushed it", exclamation marks, emoji, or cheerleading.
- **Two kinds of text, two different jobs:**
  - **World text** (Part A): the rooms, passages, camps and glimpses ahead. Rewrite these fully and richly, within the length given for each.
  - **Interface text** (Part B): buttons, labels, prompts. These are read at a glance on a phone and many are tapped. Keep them **short and plain**; you may improve the voice, but a button must stay a few words and say clearly what tapping it does. Where a line mentions a **real-world job** (e.g. "Open the vet's page on your phone"), it must stay plain, practical and literal: it is an instruction for real life, not for the game.
- Keep every `{placeholder}` in curly brackets exactly as written; the app fills them in (for example `{job}` becomes "Spanish study", `{min}` becomes "25 minutes", `{place}` becomes "the Rib Gallery").
- Respect the length limit given for each line. Room text sits over a painting on a phone screen, so it can be several sentences, not a page.

### How to return it

- After each Part, you may add up to five bullet points headed **Reviewer notes** about the writing only (voice, consistency, anything you changed across the board). Nothing about the plot.

## How a day plays, in order (for context)

1. **Today** (opening screen): the name of the place where the expedition is, a line of what lies **Ahead**, and Dan's next real job with **Begin**.
2. **Setting up a delve**: pick how many timed sessions and how long; a line shows where this will take the expedition.
3. **The delve**: a slowly moving tunnel painting while Dan works on the real job, then a short screen when time is up.
4. **A step**: a job done away from the phone (the gym, cooking) moves the expedition a little; a short line describes the passage walked.
5. **Arrival**: when the day's jobs are done, the expedition arrives: a named chamber (first time) or a camp for the night.
6. **"I can't start"**: if Dan is stuck, a glimpse of what's just ahead, one tiny first physical step, and "10 minutes?".

The expedition's route so far (throwaway sample places, used to try the game out): **The Well Stair** (the start) → **The Rib Gallery** (after 75 minutes of delving) → **The Pool Dome** (after 275 minutes).
