# D — The combined look: critique round 2

_Reviewer: new eyes, harsher than round 1. Sources: `shots/*.jpg`; fresh screenshots at 390×844 (cut at 1.5, 4, 5, 6, 7.2 and 9 s; complete at 1.5 and 4 s; `morning#done`, `delve#breather`, `map#stair`) and at 360×780 (all eleven screens); the HTML, `direction.css` and `hall.js`. `delve-set-variants.html` was not reviewed._

## Verdict

The interface is now close to finished. The Low / Normal / High boxes, "NEXT ——", the purple button, the "Today · · Map" top bar and the gold turn at the arrival all read as one app that belongs to Dan, and every screen keeps its one action. The painting is where it falls short, and in places the revision has moved it backwards. `hall.js` replaced the C hall that Dan said he loved with a low-poly 3D render of a narrow corridor. That is close to the "3D and lit" look he turned down (5D), and he has not yet seen it. The Salt Gallery reads as a stage curtain with smoke rising up it. The stair still reads as going up. The new delve-length dial is attractive, but its scale is wrong, and at 45 minutes it sends Dan past a sealed lintel.

## Blockers (must fix)

1. **delve-set.html: the dial's scale is wrong.** The four stops are a quarter-turn apart, 25 at 3 o'clock, 30 at 6, 45 at 9 and 60 at 12. So the lit arc shows 25 minutes as a quarter of the ring and 30 minutes as half. The first five minutes take a quarter-turn, and so does the step from 45 to 60. "60" also sits at 12 o'clock, where the ring starts, so the start and the maximum share a label. The delve ring (D-028) uses fill to mean time, so the dial that sets it has to as well. **Fix:** make the angle proportional to a 60-minute face: 12 o'clock is 0, 25 → 150°, 30 → 180°, 45 → 270°, 60 → 360°. Put the stop labels at those angles, 30 px outside the ring. Keep the detent and magnet feel. Nothing else on the dial changes.
2. **delve-set.html: the route goes somewhere the world does not allow.** At 45 minutes the words say "Further · The head of the stair · past the Salt Gallery, to where the stair begins", and the route bar draws a stair glyph. By the shared content and D's own map, the stair lies **beyond the lintel**, which is sealed and needs a word. Time alone must not carry Dan past a seal. At 60 it invents "Somewhere unlit", which is geography nobody has decided (rule 6). **Fix:** keep every reach on the open route: 25 "Towards the Salt Gallery", 30 "Into the Salt Gallery", 45 "Along the Salt Gallery", 60 "To the far end of the Salt Gallery". Use arch glyphs only, and never the stair glyph before the lintel opens.
3. **hall.js (morning, delve-set, cut, camp): the one hall is now a 3D-rendered corridor, and it replaced the painting Dan loved.** Dan said of C's morning scene, "I really do like the image in C", and he picked D for its painting. The new hall has hard polygon facets showing in the vault, stepped (aliased) edges on the lintel opening, flat-shaded blocks and a single fog blob at the vanishing point. It reads as a real-time 3D render, which is the look he rejected in sample 5D. The shape is not "a long high hall rounded like the inside of a shell": the walls stand nearly vertical, the lintel sits at head height, and the hall is barely two lintels wide, so it reads as a passage. The cup niches show as dark slabs that stick out like hanging signs (morning, upper right), and no cup is visible in any of them. **Fix:** (a) Change the proportions: raise the vault to about 3× the width and lower the camera toward the floor, so the swell of the walls reads. Put the cup line clearly below a big field of dark stone. (b) Add a painterly pass after rendering: soften the facets with a directional brush-noise blur, break up the joints, and add C's luminous fog in front of the geometry, not only at the far end. (c) Make each niche a shallow arched recess with a small dark cup shape inside it. (d) Put the old C morning next to the new one in the gallery and let Dan choose before this becomes the shared hall.
4. **complete.html: the Salt Gallery still does not read as a split, sealed salt face.** The vertical striations are even and soft, so the face looks like a drawn theatre curtain. The split is a lumpy dark column with glitter that fades out at the top into dark sky. With no top edge to the wall, it reads as smoke rising, or a rope hanging, not a crack. **Fix:** give the face a visible top (a heavy overhang or ceiling line about 12% down) and start the split at that edge. Draw the split as a crisp, sharp-lipped V: widest at the top, narrowing to a point at knee height, dark inside, with the packed stones set back **in** the crack and pale salt crusted over them. Break the curtain rhythm with horizontal bedding bands and a few cleaved facets that catch the gold.
5. **stair.html: it still reads as going up.** We see the **risers** of the treads, stacked like piano keys and climbing up-left into the light, and white triangle artefacts show under each tread. A flat landing plane cuts across the lower half along a hard horizontal seam. A stair you are about to go down shows its **tread tops and lit nosings**, and each step drops out of view. **Fix:** put the camera over the lip, pitched down about 35–40°. Draw only tread tops and nosing lines, each one lower and smaller, disappearing under a band of glowing mist. Let the light come up from below the bottom edge. Remove the riser faces and the triangle artefacts, and blend the landing into the first tread with no seam.

## Should fix

1. **morning.html: the "Ahead" label was dropped, and it was part of what Dan agreed.** The model-test agreement names "the plain 'Ahead' sentence". Without its label, "The lintel, on the side wall. It needs a word." reads as a continuation of "from last night's bedtime". The line also breaks badly ("It / needs a word."). **Fix:** restore `AHEAD ——` as a `.label-line`, matching `NEXT ——`, and break the line after "wall." (a `max-width` of about 22ch, or `<br>`). The space can come from the seg-to-note gap.
2. **morning.html → delve-set.html: the length dial is on the wrong job.** Begin on *Order the cat's medication* opens a 25–60-minute delve setter. Then "Start the delve" lands on a delve for *Course: one hour*. Meanwhile the Course row, the job that really is a delve, goes straight to `delve.html` and skips the setter. **Fix:** in the sample flow, the Course row opens `delve-set.html` with "Course: one hour" as its heading, and the cat job's Begin goes wherever an avoided small job goes (for the mock-up, straight to `delve.html` with the cat job's name, or a stub). The header and the delve screen must name the same job.
3. **camp.html: warm dots along the hall walls outside the cut.** The `sc-motes` canvas draws 18 **warm** (`rgba(255,214,160)`) motes across the full width, 40–72% down the screen. That is the cup line on both walls. At a glance they read as distant lit cups, which only the cut may show. **Fix:** make camp's motes the cool violet of morning's (`rgba(206,210,255)`), or keep any warm ones inside the lamp's halo (right third, within about 80 px of the flame).
4. **cut.html: the waking cups flare into two big orange blobs at the far end (about 6–7.5 s).** The farthest cups, closest to the vanishing point, flare larger and brighter than the middle ones. It looks like a pair of headlights or eyes at the end of the hall, which is exactly the wrong read. The final frame is fine. **Fix:** scale each cup's glow radius and peak opacity by distance (`sc`), not only its flame, and cap the far glows at a few px, so they run together into a soft line of light rather than two blobs.
5. **cut.html: at the reveal the word box covers too much of the "entire phone".** During the camera push the box stays as a 380 px dark glass panel over the lower 45% of the screen, with about 100 px of empty space under "wake / stone". Dan wanted the whole phone to be the hall and the marks "overlaid onto the whole screen". **Fix:** during the reveal (about 4.5–8 s), fade out the box's fill and edge and keep only the lit pill with its two marks, lifted to about 62% of the height. Bring the edge back with "Go through". Trim the box's bottom padding to 24 px in the final state.
6. **complete.html and record.html: the flow after the arrival.** (a) No action appears until 6.0 s, and the buttons only finish rising at 7.2 s, so on a low day Dan waits seven seconds to leave. **Fix:** a tap anywhere skips to the end state, and the buttons arrive by about 4 s. (b) "Read the tally" → record → "Done reading" / "Today" lands on `morning.html#later`, the afternoon, with a violet Begin on the next job. That quietly undoes "That's the day. Enough." **Fix:** from the arrival, the record returns to `complete.html` (or `morning.html#done`). This is a link target, not a colour change, so the round-1 reason for declining it does not apply.
7. **record.html: the reading band is a lit panel.** Dan asked for a line with no scratches, and he is right. But as built it is a flat, lighter-violet slab with a hard top edge and a bright bottom rule across the full width, which is the "enclosed box" he keeps rejecting. The upper half of the screen is also much lighter than the lower, so the page splits in two. **Fix:** cut the band into the stone instead of laying it on top: the same tone as the face, an inner shadow under the top lip, a thin lit lip along the bottom, and ends that fade out 16 px short of each screen edge. Even out the tally face so it darkens smoothly downward instead of stepping at the band.
8. **satchel.html: the bag still reads as a box.** It has a flat top plane with a bark-like grain, square corners and no flap hanging over the front. It reads as a leather-covered crate or a toaster. The ledge is a flat band with a hard horizontal edge across the screen at 46%. **Fix:** make the flap drape. It should curve over the top and hang down about 40% of the front, with a rolled, burnished edge and the buckle straps coming from under it. Round the body's bottom corners and let it slump a little. Replace the ledge's hard edge with a lit stone lip that fades into the dark.
9. **daybook.html: a different stone, and one mark that could read as a figure.** The wall is stucco-like blocks with thick, wobbly black mortar. It is a third stone style that matches neither `hall.js` nor the record face, and the body text runs across the mortar lines. The *lamp* sample (a Y-shaped stem on a dish, in `daybook.html` line 95) reads at a glance as a person with raised arms, the algiz-rune reading. **Fix:** use a close crop of the hall's stone from `hall.js` (a flat wall, camera square-on), with a scrim behind the text. Redraw *lamp* as a dish with a single flame tongue and no forked stem.
10. **delve-set.html: the route bar does not read as centred (Dan: "Make sure the bar is centred though and well aligned").** At 25 minutes the lit part ends at a third of the width. The rest is a 16%-opacity dotted line, and the later glyphs are hidden at opacity 0. The bar looks left-aligned and cut short. **Fix:** draw the whole track as a visible hairline (about 30% opacity) and show every destination glyph dimly from the start, lighting each as it is reached, so the bar's full, centred span always shows.
11. **satchel.html: breaks the one heading rule.** Every other screen names its place or object in a carved `h1` ("THE LAMP HALL", "THE LONG TALLY", "WEEK TWO"). The satchel has only a small `label-line`. **Fix:** `<h1 class="carve lg">The satchel</h1>`, with "For the days you have a list." under it.
12. **index.html: the gallery leaves out delve-set,** Dan's own new pick. The tour lists ten screens and skips from morning to delve. **Fix:** add delve-set as 02 and renumber.

## Nits

1. **stair.html:** "THE HEAD OF THE / STAIR" leaves an orphan at both widths. Use `.carve.md` (18 px) with `text-wrap: balance`, or break after "HEAD".
2. **cut.html:** the end copy repeats itself and mixes tenses: "THE LINTEL / The lintel answered. / The lamps woke down the hall, and the stone under it has opened." Try "It answered." and "The lamps woke down the hall, and the stone beneath it slid away."
3. **cut.html:** at the painting's scale the *wake* mark on the lintel (two arcs) reads as a refresh icon. Draw it with the dot and lower arc heavier, as it appears in the word box.
4. **delve.html:** the ■ in "Stop for now" is a media-player stop icon. Drop it, since the word is enough.
5. **delve-set.html:** the "25" stop label sits on the lamp's warm glow at the right edge (both widths). Frame the hall so the lamp falls outside the dial's band, or add a little more dimwash there.
6. **map.html:** there is a dead band of about 190 px between "THE GREAT DOOR" and the description box. In `#stair`, the crosshair's right ticks touch the screen edge at 390. Pull the map down 40 px and move the stair node 12 px in.
7. **record.html:** the guess words are bold italic, heavier than anything else on the screen. Use regular italic at 18 px, and bold only the chosen one.
8. **Set-wide:** the clay lamp is drawn three different ways (`hall.js`, `satchel.html`, `daybook.html`). It is one object, so use one drawing.
9. **morning.html:** the gap from "THE LAMP HALL" to the seg boxes is about 10 px, the tightest on the screen. Give it 16 px so the header breathes like camp's.

## The single change that would most improve this direction

**Make `hall.js` look painted, the way C's hall did, before anything else.** Four screens use it, plus the dial's background, and it is the thing Dan chose D for. Keep its one vanishing point and its camera moves, which make the full-screen cut work. Then rebuild its proportions into a high, swelling shell-vault and add a painterly pass (soft facets, brush noise, fog in front of the stone) so it stops reading as a 3D render. Put the old and new halls side by side for Dan before settling on it.
