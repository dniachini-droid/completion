# D — The combined look: critique round 1

_Reviewer: fresh eyes, harsh. Sources: `shots/*.jpg`, re-shots at 390×844 (cut at 1.5 s / 4 s / 5.5 s / 9 s, complete at 1.5 s) and at 360×780 (all ten screens), and the HTML/CSS._

## Verdict

The interface layer is strong. B's boxes, the "NEXT ——" label lines, the Low / Normal / High selector and the purple main button now sit on one centred grid, and every screen has one obvious action. The set is calm and has no red or counts. It does feel like one app. The weak part is the painting, which Dan was choosing for. The hall looks like a tiled railway tunnel, not a shell-shaped hall of cut stone. The salt face looks like rippled water seen through an oval window. The stair reads as steps going up. The cut, Dan's favourite moment, plays in the top-left quarter of the screen while the interface stays put. It is not the full-screen shot he asked for. So the controls are faithful to Dan's words, but the world he picked does not yet match them.

Hard checks: no red, no counts of undone things, no badges, streaks, XP or bars, and no guilt or cheerleading. Wall-cups are dark everywhere except the cut. One near-miss on "no figure" (Blocker 4). The morning screen carries all of the agreed information. The delve meets D-028.

## Blockers (must fix)

1. **cut.html: the big moment is not full-screen, which Dan asked for explicitly ("I want it to just be the entire phone").** At 5.5 s the reveal is a navy shutter sliding down inside a box in the top-left quarter. Meanwhile the header ("Cut the word" is still showing after the word has locked), the word pill and "Later" all stay fixed. The camera never moves, so you never "see all the way down the hallway" the way B's version let you. **Fix:** once the second mark is cut, fade out the top bar, the heading and the choice row (about 300 ms). Let the painting take the whole phone and push the camera slowly down the hall (scale plus translate on the painting layer, 2.5–3 s) while the cups wake one after another. Bring the word back as an overlay in the lower third with the heading "The lintel answered." Only then fade in "Go through". Make the lintel opening a dark recess in cut stone with light spilling from below, not a flat blue rectangle with shutter lines.
2. **complete.html: the Salt Gallery does not read, and it sits in a window.** The "salt face" is a repeating ripple texture that looks like water or hammered metal. Faint large triangles show through it like leftover geometry. The whole scene sits in an oval spotlight with a hard black edge across the top corners, which is exactly the "scene in a window" Dan rejected four times. The split starts mid-wall rather than at the top, and nothing shows "knee height". **Fix:** paint the face as a tall crystalline wall that bleeds off every edge: vertical salt striations, sparkle at the ridges, no oval mask. Run the split from the top edge of the frame down to about a quarter of the wall's height above the floor line, with a floor edge or step to give it scale. Remove the triangle layer. Keep the purple-to-gold sweep, which works.
3. **stair.html: the painting says "up", not "down".** A raised bank of steps rises towards a lit door at the vanishing point. That reads as a stair climbing away, or a dais. The landing is a flat purple floor with one white rule across it. The vault is drawn as a hard wire grid, a different hand from the morning, cut and camp halls. **Fix:** put the viewer on the landing looking over the lip. Show the first three or four treads dropping below the lower frame edge, the side walls continuing down, and the light coming from below, not from a door straight ahead. Redraw the vault in the same painted style as `morning.html`'s hall.
4. **morning.html, cut.html: a lintel mark reads as a little walking person.** The second carved mark on the lintel (the "ᛣ"-like shape beside the "F" shape) looks at a glance like a stick figure mid-stride. "No figure anywhere" is a hard story rule, and a figure-like mark on the very lintel the player is sent to could be read as a planted clue. **Fix:** swap it for an abstract sample shape with no head-and-limbs reading (e.g. a hooked bar or a notched square). Check every sample mark in the set with the same eye.

## Should fix

1. **The hall (morning, cut, camp): it looks like a subway tunnel, not "a long high hall rounded like the inside of a shell, cut stone".** The walls are a uniform grid of small brick-sized tiles with regular ribs, the section is a plain semicircle, and the scale feels like a pedestrian underpass. **Fix:** fewer, much larger blocks with visible joints and chisel texture. Taper the vault into a slightly pointed, swelling section (shell-like, taller than it is wide). Add height: the cup line at about a third of the wall, empty dark stone above. Paint soft haze and bloom over the geometry so the line-work stops showing.
2. **morning.html: the lintel reads like a signboard or ticket box, and the header stack squeezes the painting.** Day, place name, three selector boxes, the note and the "Ahead" sentence together take 540 of 844 px before the painting. The lintel is a pale slab with glyphs on a strip, and the reticle brackets around it look like a game HUD target. One dark cup floats in mid-air above the lamp, attached to nothing. **Fix:** tighten the header: 8 px less above the selector, put "from last night's bedtime" on the same line as "AHEAD"'s rule, or drop the "Ahead" label and keep the sentence. Paint the lintel as a heavy stone beam set into the wall over a blank, with the marks cut into it. Keep the corner brackets fine and faint, at about half their current opacity. Seat the floating cup on the cup line.
3. **map.html: the paths are B-bright and the labels collide.** Dan asked for A's look ("very nebulous, and how the areas you've seen are lit up"). The routes are continuous bright hairlines, and the Mouth-to-Great-door line runs straight through "THE SALT GALLERY". The Lamp Hall label sits hard against the crosshair. "THE SURVEY CUT" floats far from its node. Seen places (Salt Gallery, Great door) glow almost as bright as walked ones, which undoes "areas you've seen are lit up". **Fix:** let the lines draw in (B), then settle to A's dotted dust at about 35% opacity. Move the Salt Gallery label below its node and to the right of the line. Put a 14 px gap between the Lamp Hall label and the crosshair. Dim seen-not-reached nodes to cold pinpoints and keep the bloom for walked places.
4. **The set's headings and top bars do not follow one rule (family).** Most screens head with carved caps ("THE LAMP HALL", "THE LONG TALLY"), but cut ("Cut the word", "The lintel answered.") and daybook ("Week 2", in cream) head in Spectral. The top-right slot holds MAP, DAYBOOK, AGAIN, a bare replay icon, a centred "THE MAP" title or nothing. Stair's Map icon is a different glyph from morning's. **Fix:** one rule. Place and object names are carved caps in `.carve.lg`. Plain-print headlines only for sentences the app says to Dan ("The lintel answered." is fine as the sentence under a carved "THE LINTEL"; "Week 2" should become carved "WEEK TWO" with the date line under it). The top-right slot is always either Map or empty. Use morning's map icon everywhere.
5. **record.html: the primary button is the wrong action and goes to the wrong place.** "DONE READING" is the loudest, glowing element on a screen whose job is to pick a guess. It also links to `morning.html` (the dawn state, "Begin: order the cat's medication"), although the natural way in is from day complete. The screen stays cold violet after the day has turned gold. **Fix:** make the four choices the focus (the selected one lit in `.box.lit`), turn "Done reading" into a `.btn-quiet` or text link, send it back to where you came from (`complete.html`, or `morning.html#done`), and add `s-done` when you arrive after the arrival.
6. **record.html: the mark row is uneven and the scene is wallpaper.** The "a name" caption sits about 12 px lower than the others. The unknown last mark's caption is a single "·" that looks like a stray pixel. The background is the same tally scratch repeated in rows, which reads as a tiled pattern and competes with the real marks. The band between the line and "THIS MARK" (about 250 px) is empty. **Fix:** put all captions on one baseline. Give the unknown mark an em-rule caption ("—") in `--ink-3`. Vary the scratches in size, density and angle, fade them out towards the edges, and blur the rows furthest from the reading line. Pull "THIS MARK" up by about 80 px, or move the reading line down to sit at the optical centre.
7. **satchel.html: the satchel is still clip-art, and the "glow" looks like a glitch.** It has flat brown fills, dashed-line stitching and cartoon buckles. The glowing animation is a thin purple line across the buckles that reads as a scan line. The right strap runs off the edge of the screen and down behind the list heading. **Fix:** paint the leather with worn highlights, darker creases and rim light from the lamp. Put the glow inside, leaking softly from under the flap (a warm or violet haze that breathes). Bring the strap back into the frame, looped on the ledge.
8. **camp.html: the lamp you are camping by is half off the screen.** The flame and the dish are clipped at the right edge, so the warm source that defines camp is cropped. **Fix:** move the ledge and lamp in by about 60 px so the whole dish and halo sit inside the right third, and let its warmth reach the lower-right of the text block.
9. **Small carved labels.** `.label-line`, `.label`, `.topbar .title` and the nav row are 13 px Cinzel caps in `--ink-3` / `--ink-2`. This is below the ~16 px bar for text that matters, and the nav row (Satchel, Daybook, Camp) matters. **Fix:** set the nav row and top-bar links to 15 px, and section labels to 14 px with the tracking reduced to .2em, so the widths hold at 360 px (there is room: at 360 the nav row has about 30 px spare).

## Nits

1. **complete.html, cut.html:** the "AGAIN" button and the replay icon are mock-up controls in the product's top bar. Move them to the gallery page, or to a small control off-canvas.
2. **map.html:** "THE MAP" top-bar title plus "THE FIRST REGION / THE QUIET" is two headings for one screen. Drop the top-bar title.
3. **delve.html:** the filled arc is only slightly brighter than the empty track. D-028 says the ring fills "with glow". Thicken the filled arc to about 3 px, add a soft outer bloom that grows with the fraction, and let the inside of the ring pick up a faint wash as it fills. The tunnel is C's blur, which Dan loved, so keep it, but a touch more contrast on the nearest two ribs would make "going through" read at a glance.
4. **daybook.html:** the brick wall is regular garden brickwork, not the hall's cut stone. Use the same stone as the hall, at a larger scale. "WEEK 1" is an inert `<button>`, so link it to something, even a stub.
5. **satchel.html:** the struck-through "take the bottles out" in violet on dark is borderline. Raise it to `--ink-3` for the text and keep the violet only for the rule.
6. **cut.html:** at 1.5 s "Now the second." is centred while the heading is left-aligned. Align it to the column, or centre the whole choice block including the label.
7. **record.html:** the offered guesses (rope, bread, carry) turn reading into multiple choice. That is a game-design question, not an art one. Flag it for Dan and don't settle it silently here.

## The single change that would most improve this direction

**Paint one real hall and use it everywhere.** Make a single properly painted Lamp Hall (tall, shell-shaped vault, large cut blocks, a heavy carved lintel, the clay lamp on its ledge, dark cups on one line, haze over the geometry) as the shared backdrop for morning, cut, camp and stair. Then make the cut a camera move into that same painting, full-screen. Four of the ten screens use this hall, and it is what Dan picked C for. The interface on top of it is already right.
