# Direction A: Lamp and Stone (critique, round 2)

_Reviewed fresh from new screenshots at 390×844 and 360×780, from the cut held at 1.5 s, 4 s and 9 s, from the arrival at 1.5 s, 4 s and 9 s, from `delve.html#breather` and `delve.html?job=cat`, and from the HTML and CSS. Checked against `ART_DIRECTION.md` (the brief and spec A), `PHASE4_RUN.md`, `ANTI_FEATURES.md` and `DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md`. After that I checked `CRITIQUE-1.md` and `REVISION-1.md` to see what landed._

## Verdict

The system around the pictures is now good. The layout holds at 360, the type is carved and readable, the delve ring is the best timer in the run, and nothing on any screen nags. The pictures still aren't good enough, and in this direction the pictures are the product ("the world is the screen"). The Lamp Hall is a straight box corridor with sunburst seams, not a shell-like vault. The cut, which is the cinematic moment, opens on something that looks like a toolbar with a search field and ends with a flat black slab stuck on a receding wall. The arrival is a cartoon brick wall with a hole onto a sky full of triangles. The painting quality also splits the set in two: the soft, atmospheric screens (map, delve, record) and the flat, outlined vector screens (arrival, daybook ladder, the cut's close-up, the lamp) don't look like one family. On the morning screen, warm has spread to eight things, which weakens A's own rule of one warm thing.

## Blockers

1. **Morning, cut, camp (and the index): the Lamp Hall still isn't a long, high hall rounded like the inside of a shell.**
   - **What's wrong:** It reads as a square corridor. The walls and floor are flat planes, and straight seams radiate from the vanishing point like a sunburst, so there's no curve anywhere. The wall-cups are small dark crescents strung in two converging arcs across the upper vault. At phone size they read as bunting or eyebrows, not recesses at head height on the walls. The lintel on the morning is a dark slab floating off the right wall with a pale vertical banner under it, and it isn't in the wall's perspective. The great door is a flat grey tombstone. This backdrop carries A's whole argument and appears on three screens, and C's version of the same hall (a lit, painted barrel) is visibly better.
   - **Fix:**
     - Draw the section as a tall pointed-to-round barrel: the walls lean in from about a third of the height, and there is no floor-to-wall corner.
     - Replace the radial seams with horizontal courses that follow the curve. They should be soft and broken, darker in the joints and lit on the lower lip.
     - Put the cups on the walls at head height (about 55–60% of the wall's height), recessed and each with a shadowed interior. Keep them large enough near the viewer (18–24 px) that they read as holes in stone, fading to specks down the hall. No dark crescents.
     - Seat the lintel *in* the right wall's perspective plane, as a lintel over a recessed blind doorway that shares the wall's vanishing lines.
     - Give the great door depth: jambs, a lit threshold, and cold light spilling round it.

2. **Cut: the big moment is the least crafted screen in the set.**
   - **What's wrong at 1.5 s:** The lintel is a flat blue-grey band with a dark rounded slot, so it looks like a toolbar with a search field. "wake?" and "stone?" float under the slot, not in it. "Cut the word" first appears dim and grey, so it looks disabled. There's no back link on the close-up. The wall below is flat outlined brickwork.
   - **What's wrong when settled (4 s and 9 s):**
     - The opened doorway is a front-facing black rectangle stuck on a wall that recedes at 30°. It hangs below the floor line, and its "stair" is six orange bars.
     - The woken cups are identical flame-on-dish icons, strung across the vault like fairy lights.
     - The two marks in the caption are bright line icons, although A's rule is that the Cut "never draws lines".
   - **Fix:**
     - Close-up: draw the lintel as a thick stone beam with a chamfered front, grain and chips. Make the blank a rod-shaped groove cut *into* the beam, with an inner shadow and no raised pill border. Sit "wake? · stone?" inside the groove as faint warm ghosts where the marks will go.
     - Have the button fade in at full warmth, never grey.
     - Settled view: project the opening onto the wall plane. It needs a trapezoid that matches the wall's vanishing lines, a reveal on the near jamb, and steps that foreshorten and dim into cold air, with lamplight on the first two treads only.
     - Show the woken cups as soft glows inside the recesses from B1, varying in size and warmth with distance, with no flame icons.
     - Render the caption marks as the lit grooves used on the lintel, not strokes.

3. **Arrival: the Salt Gallery reads as a cartoon brick wall with a hole onto a snowy sky.**
   - **What's wrong:** The blocks are flat rectangles with black outlines (a 2D platformer tile set). The "salt" is a pale-blue sky gradient scattered with grey triangles. The crack runs down that sky, so it doesn't split a face; the eye reads it as a crack in the air. The cairn is four smooth brown ellipses (a spa-stone stack). This is the reward screen for "That's the day. Enough." It should be among the most beautiful, and it's the least.
   - **Fix:**
     - Drop the brick wall and the hole. Paint the whole upper two thirds as the salt face itself: a rough, massive, crystalline surface. Build it from dark grey-rose rock salt with pale, translucent veins, lit cold from below, and use low-frequency noise and highlights on facets rather than triangles.
     - Paint the split as a dark, deep fissure from the top edge that narrows to nothing at knee height, packed with stones and salt (the location says it was sealed from the other side).
     - Build the cairn from irregular, angular, flat stones in the same cold rock with a warm rim from the lamp. Nothing should be smooth or brown.

## Should fix

1. **Morning: warm is spread across eight things, and the "one warm thing" rule is lost.**
   - **What's wrong:** Eight things are warm: the lamp, the "NEXT" eyebrow, the 17.5 px amber teaser, Begin, the done dot, "done this morning", the capacity dot and the nav dot. The teaser in particular competes with Begin for the eye.
   - **Fix:** Set the teaser in `--ink-2`, italic, or in cold-hi, since the teaser is the *place* talking. Set "NEXT" in `--ink-3`. Set the capacity and nav dots in ink-white. Keep warm for the lamp, Begin, and the done job only. That puts A's "warm is you" back in charge.

2. **All screens: the cold is grey-teal murk, not light, and it ignores the strongest taste signal so far.**
   - **What's wrong:** `--cold` #9fc4d8 over #111a21 fog reads as dull slate. Dan "really loves the purple light" in C.
   - **Fix:** Try a blue-violet cold for the *place's light only*: the far-end glow, the sealed things' halos and the cold dust, at about #a8b4f0 and #7f8ee0 (well below C's saturation). Keep the stone near-neutral and the warm unchanged. This keeps A distinct, because the *meaning* (warm is you) is what separates it from C, not the hue. Show it as a variant of the morning screen so Dan can compare.

3. **Every picture: the set has two rendering styles, and the flat one looks generated.**
   - **What's wrong:** The map, delve and record are soft and atmospheric. The arrival's blocks, the cut's wall below the lintel, the daybook's ladder, the satchel's bag and the clay lamp are vector shapes with uniform outlines and a blur filter.
   - **Fix:** Apply one rule everywhere: no outlined shapes. Build every object from value (light and dark masses), then texture (grain), then a lit edge on the side facing its light source. Give the lamp a real silhouette: a lower, rounder body, a pinched nozzle, a visible chip on the rim, and soot above the nozzle at a size you can see at 390 px. At the moment it still reads as a gravy boat or genie lamp.

4. **Record: this is fog, not a stone face.**
   - **What's wrong:** The spec says "marks cut into a stone face, cold", but the background is a stormy cloudscape. "This one is new. What might it be?" doesn't point at anything, so you have to hunt for which mark it means.
   - **Fix:**
     - Paint a cold, dressed stone face with the line's grooves cut into it, and the lone ring above the tally (per `LOCATIONS.md`).
     - Put a small warm caret or a soft warm underglow beneath the invited mark, and move the question directly under it.

5. **Record: three warm pools look like three primary buttons.**
   - **What's wrong:** "rope", "bread" and "morning" each sit in a glowing pool identical in weight to Begin elsewhere.
   - **Fix:** Show the candidates as plain warm text at 20 px with a faint underline or a 1 px warm dot beneath, and only the tapped one swells. Also make confirmed marks (*hand*) non-guessable: the aria label currently says "Tap to guess".

6. **Satchel: the warm primary is "Add a line", which invites admin.**
   - **What's wrong:** Under A's rule, warm is the thing you're about to *do*. Adding list items is productivity theatre (P9, P15). The finds in the side niches are grey slabs you can't read. The bag's pointed handle still reads as a handbag.
   - **Fix:**
     - Make "Add a line" a quiet cold text button.
     - Either have no warm button, or make the warm action "Take one into today" on the list.
     - Enlarge the niches' objects and light them warm from below, since finds are yours. The tin box needs a lid seam and the cut strokes; the rod needs its round section and a highlight.
     - Give the satchel a flat flap and a strap that falls to one side, not an arch handle.

7. **Arrival and breather: exits that are lit, and exits labelled as unfinished.**
   - **Arrival:** "Done for now" is the warm button on a screen that has just said "That's the day. Enough." It's both an exit lit warm and a phrase implying there's more to do.
   - **Breather:** A blank brown void, the only screen with no painting at all.
   - **Fix:**
     - On the arrival, make the warm action "Read the wall" (the reward) and put a quiet "Back to camp" beside it.
     - Paint the breather as the passage with the lamp's light resting on the floor. It only needs to be the delve's scene with the ring gone.

8. **Flow: new seams.**
   - The cut's "Go through" opens the map rather than the head of the stair.
   - The cut, reached from the map's lintel, says "‹ Today".
   - The arrival's exit lands on a morning that still shows *Order the cat's medication* as NEXT after the day was declared complete.
   - `delve.html?job=cat` starts at 14:11 with the ring 43% full, and says "One delve, or less" for a job that is a phone call.
   - **Fix:** Point "Go through" at a (future) stair screen, or back to the hall with the opening lit. Label back links by where you came from. Give the morning a day-complete state, or send the arrival to camp. Start the cat delve at a fresh ring.

9. **Map: the place names are the smallest type that matters, and the fog has stray shapes.**
   - **What's wrong:** The labels are 13.5 px Cinzel caps. Large dark teal lobes with visible edges (top left, and the middle right) read as leftover shapes rather than fog. The place-lamps are the same flame-on-dish icon used for the cups, so they read as emoji at map scale.
   - **Fix:** Set the labels at 15–16 px Cinzel, or 17 px Marcellus in sentence case with the caps kept for "The Quiet" only. Blur or remove the lobes. Draw the places as soft warm points with a small halo (as the spec says, "small lamps in the dark" as light, not as drawn lamps).

10. **Daybook: the ladder is clip art in a tube.**
    - **What's wrong:** It's a uniform-stroke vector ladder hanging over concentric rings.
    - **Fix:** Paint a narrow brick-lined shaft seen from above, with the ladder's rails running out of frame and foreshortening. Only the lowest three rungs catch lamplight, and the rest are silhouettes. Break the concentric rings into irregular coursed brick.

11. **Readability: the remaining sub-16 px text carries information.**
    - **What's wrong:** "Thursday · The Lamp Hall" is 14 px caps in `--ink-3` on the lit vault. "from last night's bedtime" is 15 px. The capacity row changes the day, so its note should be readable.
    - **Fix:** Set the header at 15–16 px with "Thursday" in `--ink-2`, and the note at 16 px.

## Nits

- **Morning/camp:**
  - **Warm dust near the wall-cups.** Warm motes drift near the lamp at cup height on the left wall (the `data-lights` warm source at 96, 322 with radius 210). In a still frame they can read as a lit cup. Keep warm motes within about 80 px of the flame, or below the shelf line. The cups must be unambiguously dark outside the cut. (No lit cups were found outside `cut.html`.)
  - **The door's count.** It shows as a row of six dots, three lit, and the map repeats "••··". It's within canon, but it reads as a progress meter or loading indicator. Show it as cut notches on the door, not as UI dots.
- **Cut:** the caption says "The lamps woke down the hall". They're cups; say "The cups woke down the hall."
- **Record:** the candidate-choice mechanic (three offered meanings) isn't defined in any game doc. Mark it as a proposal in `NOTES.md` so it isn't mistaken for a decision.
- **Daybook:** "‹ Week one" sits top right with a left chevron. Put previous weeks on the left, or make the gesture a swipe with a small "Week one" on the left.
- **Delve:** the chalky scribbles on the passage walls read as graffiti. Cut them as grooves with a faint cold inner glow, as in the record.
- **Camp:** the warm recess behind the lamp is a flat rectangle, like a lit box, and the far door floats above its top edge. Seat the recess in the wall's perspective, and let a little cold from the far end stay visible.
- **Warm button underline:** the 2 px "slot" under every warm button reads as a web underline. Make it softer and shorter (30% width), or drop it.

## The single change that would most improve this direction

**Repaint the three big pictures (the Lamp Hall, the cut and the Salt Gallery) with one painting rule: no outlined shapes, only lit masses and texture, in a true shell-like vault.** The interface is already clean and correct. What stops A reading as designed rather than generated is the flat, outlined illustration on its most important screens, and the fact that it's inconsistent with the soft, atmospheric painting on the map and delve. Fix the hall first, because the cut and camp inherit it. Then spend the rest of the effort on the cut and the arrival, the two moments Dan will remember.
