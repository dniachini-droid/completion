### Early reading (Claude's, hypothesis only)
- The broad lists (1–3) got yes to nearly everything; the forced choices (4–8) are much sharper and should weigh more.
- **Light:** one warm lamp in the dark. Cyan glowing lines in the place were a clear no, alone and combined. "Cold stone" plus "warm lamp" suggests cool, dark stone lit only by warm human light.

- **Tensions** (follow-ups below): the node map glows cyan but cyan glow in the world was a no; he loves Monument Valley but rejected flat vector; he loves Destiny's menus but rejected the sci-fi interface type.

### Brief for the directions (from the above)
**Settled enough to build on:**
- Dark, cool stone, and **glow everywhere**: mostly cold glow, with some warm glow (the lamp is one warm source). This overrides the earlier reading of 4B/4C.
- Painted and atmospheric texture (5C), not ink, flat vector or rendered 3D.
- A carved serif carries the voice (6A).
- Layout and interaction clean and precise: nothing wasted, satisfying to tap (the Monument Valley / Destiny answer, 9 "clean ones").
- Motion: smooth and responsive every day, cinematic only at big moments (8B + 8C).
- The map as places of light on dark (7E).

**Open, to be tested visually across the directions (Dan answered "not sure"):**
- How the cold and warm glow are balanced and what each one means (e.g. cold for the place, warm for you and what you've earned). Show options.
- Whether carved letters also set buttons, times and menus, or a quieter companion face does (not the geometric sans he rejected).

## Directions

_Written 2026-09-24 by the visual run (`PHASE4_RUN.md`). All three sit inside the brief above: dark cool stone, glow everywhere, painted and atmospheric, a carved voice, clean precise layout, smooth everyday motion with cinematic big moments, a map of lit places on dark. They differ in what the light **means**, how it is **drawn**, how far the **carved letters** reach, and how the interface **sits** on the world. Screens: `design/directions/`._

### How they differ at a glance
| | A — Lamp and Stone | B — Engraved Light | C — The Turning Day |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold and warm** | Cold is the place; **warm is you** and what you've earned | Almost all cold; **warm is rare**: only the clay lamp and the one next action | The glow **follows the day**: cold for work (morning, delve), warming through the arrival into camp |
| **How glow is drawn** | Soft points and haze; no lines | **Fine lines of light**, engraved and exact (tests the "cold lines" no, done precisely) | Luminous fog and bloom; painted light |
| **Carved letters** | **Everywhere**: titles, buttons, times, body text | Carved for titles and buttons; a **quiet book serif** for sentences | **Split by who speaks**: carved for the place; plain print for your own life |
| **The interface** | The world is the screen; a few words sit on the painting | An **instrument**: framed plates on a strict grid (Destiny, Mass Effect) | **One thing at a time**: full-screen moments, a single card of light |
| **Map** | Lit places like lamps in the dark; warm where you've been | A **node map** joined by engraved lines, like a star chart | Places as lights seen from above, through drifting fog |

### A — Lamp and Stone
- **Mood.** You in a vast cold hall, holding the only warm thing. Hollow Knight's hush, Arrival's grey light.
- **Materiality.** Painted blue-grey stone, soft-edged, with dust in the light. Lamps are flames. The Cut glows faintly cold in the stone and never draws lines.
- **Light as meaning.** Cold (pale blue-white, about #9fc4d8) is the place: the far end, sealed things, marks not yet known. Warm (amber, about #f0a24a) is you: the lamp, the button you're about to press, jobs done (lit cups), cairns, finds. By the end of a good week the screen has visibly more warm in it.
- **Typography.** Carved letters everywhere: Cinzel for names and buttons, Marcellus (a carved face with lower case) for every sentence, times and the daybook. It tests whether carved letters can set the whole interface.
- **World presentation.** Every screen is a painted view of a place, and the words sit in its darkest part.
- **UI philosophy.** As few words as possible, placed on the painting. No cards and no boxes. One warm button.
- **Map.** Places as small lamps in the dark, joined only by faint dust-paths. Where you've been glows warm; places seen but not reached glow cold; the unknown is dark.
- **Finds and the satchel.** Things rest in niches cut into the wall, each lit from below.
- **Motion.** The lamp breathes. Taps answer with a warm swell. The big moments are slow pull-backs of the camera and lamps waking in sequence.
- **Records.** Marks cut into a stone face, cold. Your guesses appear beneath them in warm, with a question mark. A ring glows around a name.
- **The delve ring.** A thin ring of warm light slowly closing around the time left, with the lamp's light moving down the passage behind it.

### B — Engraved Light
- **Mood.** Cold, exact, grand: an ancient instrument you are learning to operate. Destiny's director, Mass Effect's galaxy map, Andor's precision, the Prometheus halls.
- **Materiality.** Dark basalt-blue stone, polished, with lines of cold light cut into it: fine, crisp, and always straight or true arcs. Painted depth behind, exact geometry in front.
- **Light as meaning.** Cold (ice cyan-white, about #bfefff) is everything by default, the place and the interface alike. Warm is rare and precious: the clay lamp, and the single next action. It tests "glow everywhere, mostly cold" at its purest.
- **Typography.** Cinzel for titles and buttons, set small and widely spaced. EB Garamond, a quiet book serif (not the sans he rejected), for sentences and lists. It tests carved letters paired with a companion face.
- **World presentation.** The world sits in a framed viewport, like looking through an instrument.
- **UI philosophy.** A strict grid of framed plates with hairline borders, corner ticks and aligned edges. Satisfying, exact taps. Clean the way Destiny's menus are clean.
- **Map.** A node map. Places are glowing nodes, joined by engraved lines of light, with the sealed nodes shown by the shape of what they need. You zoom into a node like a galaxy map.
- **Finds.** A grid of glowing plates like an index of artefacts. Each find is drawn in fine light-line.
- **Motion.** Lines draw themselves in, and panels slide on exact curves. At big moments the light runs along every engraved line at once.
- **Records.** The record appears as an engraved panel of marks. Tapping a mark draws its guess out on a leader line, like an annotation.
- **The delve ring.** An engraved ring with fine tick marks; a line of cold light travels round it and fills it. The time left sits in the middle in carved numerals.

### C — The Turning Day
- **Mood.** The day as a journey from cold to warm. Journey's warmth arriving at the end, and Annihilation's shimmer in the air.
- **Materiality.** Painted, soft, deep: stone half lost in luminous fog. Light blooms and spills. The richest painting of the three.
- **Light as meaning.** The temperature of the glow follows the day. Morning and delves are cold blue-violet (about #8fa8ff), and the air is clear. The arrival turns gold, and camp is fully warm. It tests warm as the reward of a completed day, rather than something always present.
- **Typography.** Split by who is speaking. The place (place names, marks, record lines, what the walls say) is in carved Forum and Cinzel. Your own life (jobs, buttons, times, the satchel, the daybook) is in Spectral, a clear, plain printed serif. You can hear two voices: the stone's and the app's own. It tests carved letters reserved for the world.
- **World presentation.** Full-bleed painting on every screen, with nothing framed.
- **UI philosophy.** One thing at a time. A single card of light floats in the fog carrying the one thing to do; everything else is a swipe or a tap away. It's the calmest of the three on a low day.
- **Map.** Seen from above through drifting fog: places as pools of light, and the fog thins where you've walked.
- **Finds.** Each find is a single full-screen moment, then rests on a softly lit shelf.
- **Motion.** The fog drifts and the light blooms when you tap. The big moments are colour changes across the whole screen: cold turning to gold.
- **Records.** The record line floats large across the fog. Your guess sits under each mark in plain print.
- **The delve ring.** A soft ring of cold light that brightens as it fills, with the fog drifting through it. At the end the ring blooms.

### D — The combined look (from Dan's walkthrough, D-032)
_Dan's picks, screen by screen, put together. This is the direction being built now: `directions/d-combined/`._

**The rule in one line:** C's purple world, full screen and never framed; B's interface on top of it; A's map; the day turning from purple to gold as in C.

- **The world (from C).** Full-bleed painted scenes in purple / blue-violet light, nebula-like cloud and haze, deep views down the hall. **Never a scene inside a box or window** (Dan, four times). Gold arrives through the day: a trace in the morning, gold at the arrival, warm at camp, but camp keeps the depth of the hall (less gold, more hall).
- **The interface (from B).** Controls in crisp boxes with fine light edges: square-ish buttons, a label with a short line beside it ("NEXT ——"), Low / Normal / High as selectable boxes, small line icons beside Map, Satchel, Daybook, Camp. **Everything aligned to one grid and centred properly** (Dan noticed B's icons were off). Content (text, records, marks) sits out in the open on the scene, not in panels. **The main button is purple and glowing** (C's purple button), except at the arrival and camp, where the day has turned gold.
- **Type.** B's carved capitals for labels and buttons; C's plain print for sentences and Dan's own jobs; carved place names.
- **Morning.** C's scene and purple, B's buttons, boxes, icons and "NEXT ——" label, C's Map link. Information as agreed from the model test.
- **Delve.** C's tunnel with drifting nebula cloud (it should visibly move) and the glowing ring. "Towards the Salt Gallery" more prominent: where you're going is the headline.
- **Map.** A's layout and look (nebulous, places you've seen lit up), B's behaviour (lines draw themselves in; tap a place and a crosshair appears on it, and its description comes up in a box underneath), C's purple colours.
- **Record.** B's way of working (tap a mark to select it, clean guess choices), C's light and glow, out in the open, no enclosing panels.
- **Cutting a word.** B's animation (the door opens, the lamps wake one by one and you see all the way down the hall) and B's word shown in a box, but full screen, no window, purple; the marks overlaid on the scene.
- **Day complete, camp, satchel, daybook.** C's versions. Camp: more depth, less gold. Satchel: more realistic, with a gentle glowing animation.

### Which open question each one tests
- **Cold and warm balance:** A has warm for you, B has warm as rare, C lets the day decide.
- **Carved letters:** A uses them everywhere, B pairs them with a companion face, C reserves them for the place.
- **Cold lines:** only B uses them, done precisely, so Dan can see whether the "no" was about the lines or about how they were drawn.
