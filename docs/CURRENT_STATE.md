Good morning. Here is what happened while you slept, with no answers given away.

**Research first** (`narrative/RESEARCH.md`). What makes long mysteries work and why some go nowhere (planned endings vs improvised ones: *Babylon 5*, *Dark* and *Fullmetal Alchemist* against *Lost* and *The X-Files*); how decipherment games teach a script (*Heaven's Vault*, *Chants of Sennaar*, *Outer Wilds*, *Obra Dinn*, *Tunic*, and how real scripts were cracked); what exactly makes your favourites hit (*Eternal Darkness*, *Three-Body*, *Arrival*, *Mass Effect*, *Ocarina*, Stargate, Star Wars); and how to tell a story in fragments without anyone waiting on you. It ends in 41 binding lessons.

**Three pitches** (`narrative/PITCHES.md`), genuinely different:
1. **The Long Answer.** A quiet cut-stone place under our own world; a script that does things when read; people from different ages who each learned a few marks and each met the same tall figure; a work the makers began that takes longer than an age to finish.
2. **The Unmade.** A mountain hollowed by a people who caught something that fell and could not kill it, so they wrote it down: the script *is* the thing, taken apart; anyone who learned a word could do what it was.
3. **The Last Run.** An engine under the world that restarts the ages; the records are minds it kept from each run; the script is its control language; the one who decides when the next run begins has decided yours is nearly over.

**Chosen: The Long Answer (D-016, provisional).** It is the pitch whose ending is built into its premise, whose lives are pieces of one thing rather than episodes, whose antagonist can be revealed slowly over a year and has a reason a sane person could hold, and whose fiction never makes your progress feel like damage. It is also the least like any single one of your favourites while touching all of them.

**What now exists (sealed, in `narrative/sealed/`; you don't read it):** the full hidden truth and a fixed ending, written first; a capped question ladder with every mystery's answer, false explanations and clues; a twelve-month revelation map with ten model-changing reveals on the calendar and **week-by-week beats for all 52 weeks**, the ending included; the complete script system (about 50 marks by year end, how they combine, nine words that are powers, the first in week 2–3, partial marks, a dozen extra two-mark experiments the place answers, and eleven planned moments where an old record re-reads); every record for the first quarter, and every short line cut beside an object, written as an actual string of marks with its rendering, plus the fuller tellings behind them; the place's geography and every gate's needs; the antagonist in depth, with his one speech; five lives across the ages plus a chorus, the secondary lives given desires, secrets and their turn of the central story; a clue ledger with the truth behind every clue the first playable plants; **the whole year visit by visit as the actual lines the app will show** (every arrival, step, small choice, camp line, morning line, teaser and week-close for all 52 weeks, the ending included; each of the four regions was then read by a fresh critic as you would play it, one minute at a time, and rewritten to that reading); the contents of every sealed niche for the whole year; a pacing ledger against the Phase 2 content rate; a fair-play audit of every reveal, done twice (against the plan, then against the actual visit text, which caught several places the app would have given an answer away early: all closed); and an adversarial review (fourteen independent passes plus mine: consistency, quality, fair play, and four "played as Dan" critiques covering the whole year) with every fix applied.
